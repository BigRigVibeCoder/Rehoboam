## 0. What this contract fixes, in one paragraph

A family annal is plain text rendered from a sealed channel-A chronicle by a fixed set of templates, one sentence per admitted event of an in-scope kind, in `EventId` order under year headings, grouped by house, by family line or by year, and opened, for a house whose past is in the genesis keyframe only, by a short prologue rendered from the keyframe's life-course and tenure rows. A template is a sentence with slots, and a slot binds only to what the event carries: its roles, its anchored snapshots, its integer payload (through a layout that quotes the emitting system's `[[emits]]` clause exactly), its causal edges and its tick, plus one declared exception, the designation of a person, which reads the names file and the life-course rows (§4). A template never reads world state and never invents a fact; the lint refuses a slot outside that vocabulary by name (§7). A person is designated by the manifest's name when there is one, else by birth order among a named parent's children of the same sex, else by a compact label, `Founder #k` or `p-` and a hex prefix, never a raw id (§4; D49, ruled: G1a reads with this fallback, and a plane-C name generator comes at P2.8). Every sentence has one row in a sidecar: the event ids it shows, the persons it designates, the records it read, the template id and version; the sidecar's header names every input by digest (§8), and the self-test re-renders every group from the chronicle, the templates and the extract and compares text and sidecar in order (§12). The text and sidecar of one chronicle under one template file are byte-identical on every machine; their digest, and the manifest that lists a world's groups, is what a later plane-C fold may cover (§9). No model is called anywhere (V-NAR-07); the renderer is a pure function of its inputs (§1).

## 2. Templates and variants

`templates_v0.toml` is the whole template set: a header (`format`, `version`, `scope`), enums, payload layouts, labels, chronicle templates and prologue templates.

- **Scope** lists the kinds an annal tells, grouped as the WP names them: births (`BIRTH`), marriages (`MARRIAGE`), deaths (`DEATH`, `EXTINCTION`), successions (`SUCCEED`, `INTERREGNUM_CRISIS`, `DEPOSED`, `REGENCY_BEGUN`, `REGENCY_ENDED`, `SEAT_GRANTED`, `HOUSE_EXTINCT`), claims (`CLAIM_CREATED`, `CLAIM_PRESSED`, `CLAIM_RENOUNCED`) and feuds (`FEUD_DECLARED`, `FEUD_ENDED`). Sixteen kinds, every one declared by a system's `[[emits]]` (the lint checks). An admitted event of another kind is not rendered as a sentence; it is counted in the sidecar's trailer (§5) and may be named as a cause through its `[[label]]` (§3). The kinds T1 emits are told through their consequences in P1a (a `DEATH` with an actor is "murdered by"); P2.8 widens the scope.
- **A template** is `{ id, version, kind, when?, text, label }`. `text` is the sentence; `label` is the noun phrase another sentence uses when it names this event as a cause ("after the death of X"). For one kind the variants are tried **in file order** and the first whose `when` holds renders; the last variant of a kind has no `when` and no earlier one lacks a `when` (the lint checks both), so every admitted event of an in-scope kind renders exactly one sentence, and the choice is a function of the event alone.
- **`when`** has five keys, each a predicate on the event: `has = [roles]` (each role filled; `father` means the target's `LIFE_ENTRY` names a father), `lacks = [roles]`, `payload = { slot = enum id }` (the payload slot's code is that id), `edge = kind` (the chosen told edge of that kind exists, §3: for `motivated_by`, one with a positive weight; R40) and `motive = "named" | "unnamed"` (that same chosen `motivated_by` edge carries a Registry motive, or not: R8, R40). Nothing else is a condition: not a date, not another event, not state.
- **A `[[label]]`** is `{ kind, version, label }` for a kind out of scope that an in-scope kind's edge may point to: a `MURDER_ATTEMPT`, a `MENTAL_BREAK`, a `FLED`, every other kind an emitter declares (R3). The lint requires one for every kind any `[[emits]]` declares that is not in scope, so a cause slot always renders. A label uses the cause's roles only.
- **Versions.** `templates.version` is the set's semver; a template's or label's `version` is an integer. Any change to a text, a slot, a layout, an enum word, a label or the scope bumps `templates.version` and the changed entry's `version`, and the goldens are regenerated by the reference (`--write`, never by hand). The sidecar names both (§8).

## 3. The slot grammar (what a template may bind to)

A slot is `{name}` in a template text, parsed by the standard library's `string.Formatter` (the one parser, shared by the lint and the renderer); a conversion (`{x!r}`) or a format spec (`{x:>8}`) is refused. The names, exactly:

| Slot | Binds to | Renders as | Lint: resolves iff |
|---|---|---|---|
| `date` | the event's `tick` | `day 14 of month 3` (the year is the heading, §5) | always |
| `year` | the event's `tick` | `the twelfth year (Year of Ash)`; before the epoch, `the third year before the epoch (…)` | always |
| `actor`, `target`, `instrument` | the cast row of that role (`event_role`) | the person's designation (§4); `no one` when the role is unfilled on this event | the kind's `[[emits]] roles` lists the role |
| `witnesses` | every `witness` row | the designations, comma-joined; `no one` when there is none | `roles` lists `witness` |
| `father` | the `father_id` of the target's `LIFE_ENTRY`, written in the birth's instant | the father's designation; `no one` when the record names none | the kind is `BIRTH` (R6) |
| `payload.<slot>` | one integer of the payload, by the kind's layout | an enum's words (`by murder`), an ordinal word (`second`), or the integer; a code outside the enum is refused by name | `[payloads.<KIND>]` declares the slot, its `cited` text is the whole `Payload: [...]` clause of the emitting `[[emits]]` `when`, verbatim, with one slot per comma-separated item (F12), and `system` is the emitter |
| `snapshot.<attr>.<role>` | the `event_snapshot` row of attribute `attr` whose entity is the role's person, no partner | by the attribute's Registry type: a `q8_8` as an exact decimal (`63.0`), an enum by its value name, a bool or int as the number; `not recorded` when the row is absent (an absent owner, envelope §4 rule 4) | the kind's `snapshots` declares `{ attr, of = role }`; `of = "event"` and `witness` are undefined in v0 and refused |
| `motive` | the `motive` of the chosen `motivated_by` edge (below) | `[enums.motive]` words by `[motive_edges]` index (`out of jealousy of`); `for a motive the chronicle does not name` when the edge carries none | the kind's `motives` is non-empty |
| `cause.triggered`, `cause.contingent`, `cause.motivated_by` | the chosen told edge of that kind, its cause event | the cause's label (its variant's `label` for an in-scope kind, its `[[label]]` otherwise), rendered with the cause's own fields; the cause id joins the sentence's events. When no told edge of the kind exists but an untold one does (`cause_told = 0`), or the cause is absent from the file: `an event the chronicle does not tell`, with the earliest such cause recorded as `untold/<id>` in the sources (F15) | the slot is in a `text`, never in a `label` (no recursion) |

**Which edge is chosen.** For `triggered` and `contingent`, the earliest told edge of the kind (edges are in `cause_id` order). For `motivated_by`, a told edge is chosen only if its weight is positive: the largest positive weight, ties to the lower `cause_id` (R20, R31). An edge of weight zero or less never moved the actor (envelope §5), so with no positive edge none is chosen, no cause is cited, and the kind's plain variant renders (`feud.declared`: "X declared a feud against Y."); `when.motive = "unnamed"` means the strongest positive edge carries no Registry motive. `motive` and `cause.motivated_by` read the same edge. (The envelope's coalescing rule in `envelope_v0.md` §5 has the same exposure; the Architect amends it through SPR-020, outside this contract.)

Three rules the grammar enforces by construction:

1. **Only the event, and designation.** Every family above reads the event's own record (the tick, `event_role`, `event_snapshot`, `causal_edge`, `payload`) or, for a cause slot, another event's record. The one declared exception is designation (§4): a role slot renders a person through the names file, the person's `LIFE_ENTRY` (or keyframe life-course row), the rows of the parent's children (for the birth order) and the `house_member` rows (for the house), every one of which the sidecar row cites (F19). No slot reads a world column, a sift match, a significance value or the manifest beyond those. A name that is not in the table, such as `state.title.holder`, `weather` or `house`, is refused by the lint as "outside the envelope" (§7). `05.11` §3's envelope derivations (time of day, lighting, inferred emotion) are not slots in v0: a future derivation is a declared function of envelope fields, added to the table with its own lint rule, never a free read.
2. **Nothing invented.** A template may add fixed words (the historian's register: D16) but no fact: no cause of death the payload does not carry, no title the event does not name, no pronoun (the chronicle carries sex only in life-course records, so a person is always designated, never "he" or "she"), no emotion and no state word ("under strain" is not in v0: R7; a state word comes back only as a declared derivation against a Registry threshold, cited in the sidecar). When an event does not carry a fact, the variant for that case says less (`death.other`: "died by accident"; `claim.created`: "gained a claim to a vacant title"; `deposed`: "was deposed", and with an actor "X standing as the leading claimant", which is what M7's timer fire records).
3. **Payloads are cited, not assumed.** The envelope fixes a payload's meaning per verb in the emitting spec's `when` text, not in a typed field (M2 0.3.0: "Payload: [sex code, birth order (parity after the birth)]"; `DEATH`: "Payload: [death_cause code]"). A layout therefore quotes that whole clause and the lint checks it against `specs/systems` (the `next/` version superseding the frozen one, as `lint_odd.py --next` does), so a layout cannot outlive the sentence it reads. A kind whose `when` states no layout has no payload slot: M7 0.3.4's verbs (and its 0.4.0 draft's) name the title in the payload without a layout, so v0's succession sentences do not name the title. This is a stopgap (R15): BCK-002 A-55 types `payload = [...]` on `[[emits]]` for M2, M7 and T1 before SPR-021, and the annals bind to the typed list then.

## 4. Designating a person (what the chronicle carries, and the fallback)

The chronicle carries **no names**: an `EVENT` names persons by `PersonId`; a `LIFE_ENTRY` carries sex, mother and father ids and the birth tick; the manifest names its cast (`cast[].name`), its dynasties (`polities[].dynasties[].name`) and its titles (`polities[].titles`), and nothing names the synthesised population, the back-filled dynasts or an in-run birth (D49, ruled 2026-10-17: a deterministic plane-C name generator at P2.8; plane A stores no name strings; G1a reads with the fallback below). One function designates a person and one function finds a house, used by the renderer, the grouping and the self-test alike (R4, R6). The rule, in order:

1. **A name**, from the names file. Source token `name/<pid>`.
2. **The Nth son or daughter of a named father**: the person's life-course row (a `LIFE_ENTRY`, else the keyframe's row) names a father who is named; N is the person's birth order among that father's children of the same sex, read from every life-course row (channel A and the keyframe) and ordered by birth tick: `the second son of Edric Ashcombe`. When two such children share a birth tick (twins), the rows cannot order them and the rule does not apply. Sources `life/<pid>` (or `kf:life/<pid>`), `name/<father>`, and the life-course rows of every child of that father of the same sex (the rank's sources, §8, R21 (b)); the father joins the sentence's persons.
3. **The Nth son or daughter of a named mother**, the same way, when the father is unnamed.
4. **A compact label, never a raw id** (R17, the director's G1a feedback on D49): a person whose keyframe row carries a founder index `k` is `Founder #k+1` (the display counts from one, R26; `founder = 0` prints `Founder #1`; source `kf:life/<pid>`); anyone else is `p-` and the shortest prefix of the id written as 16 lowercase hex digits, zero-padded, at least four digits long, that is unique among **the run's persons** (`p-aae0`): the keyframe extract's persons, every person the chronicle names in a role or a life-course record, and the parent ids of every channel-A `LIFE_ENTRY` (R28, R38; a keyframe row's parents enter only through their own rows), so a father a birth sentence names is always in the pool. A `p-` label cites nothing beyond the person's id: it adds no source to the row, where `Founder #k` cites `kf:life/<pid>` (R42). So a person's label is the same in every annal rendered from one chronicle, and it may lengthen as a live chronicle grows, which the sidecar's full id survives. The sidecar keeps the full id in every row's persons, so the label never loses provenance.

**The house walk** (R18; exactly M7's `on_birth` under the membership rule of the M7 0.5.0 draft, `house_member.house`, BCK-002 A-56, which is agnatic): a person's house is the keyframe's `house_member` row when there is one (source `kf:member/<pid>`); else, for a birth during the run (a channel-A `LIFE_ENTRY` of origin `born`, source `life/<pid>`), the father's house, found by this same rule through any number of in-run rows; else none. The mother's house never passes. There is no walk into a genesis person's ancestry: a genesis person without a `house_member` row has no house, whatever their parents' rows say (the `ashcombe` fixture's Xan, a genesis grandson of Edric through a daughter; his in-run son by Sibby, a founder of no house, walks to his father, none, and stops: no house). The in-run-father probe (PR #79's review R7): Col, born in the run to Cedric, himself born in the run to Osric, walks two in-run rows to Osric's keyframe row, House Ashcombe. The walk decides a person's group (§5), not the label. Under the frozen M7 0.3.4 the walk fell back to the mother unconditionally (the cognatic rule), which R18 followed; the draft drops the fallback, and the agnatic back-fill and this rule agree on every row (no daughter of a house bears in the back-fill).

A person is **never** designated by the birth sentence being told (the earlier "the second child of Maud" is gone: R6), and a raw twenty-digit id never appears in the text (R17). The rule goes one generation deep by name (rules 2 and 3 name a parent only when the parent is named, not described), so a designation is at most "the Nth son of X", never a chain. Disambiguation is **run-wide** (R24, R33), over the same pool as the labels: a name borne by two persons of the run is followed by its bearer's compact label wherever it designates, `Maud (p-4a8f)` and `Maud (Founder #3)`; a kin designation whose named parent bears a shared name is followed by the *designated person's own* label, the parent staying a bare name inside the phrase, `the first son of Edric Ashcombe (Founder #8)`; the label cites its source row (`kf:life/<pid>` for a founder index). So designation is injective by construction; check 9 stays as the guard, and the `shared-name` control (the disambiguation switched off) fails it. The `ashcombe` fixture carries the case: Orm, a cast member, bears Edric's name, so both fathers and every child designated through either carry a label (D49's generator makes shared names routine). Every person a sentence designates is listed in its sidecar row with the records the designation read, so a reader can audit it. A `LIFE_ENTRY` is a channel-A record, not world state: reading it is reading the chronicle (`05.10` §4's fidelity promise), and the sidecar says so.

**The hook for a generated name (D49, P2.8).** The name generator writes `[persons]` entries of the names file; rule 1 then applies, and the sidecar cites `name/<pid>` as for a cast name. The contract defines the hook, not the generator, which is not part of SPR-029. The director pre-registered that the generator moves forward into P1a if G1a's readers find the bare labels disrupt immersion; that move costs no contract change.

## 5. Grouping, ordering, headings and what is left out

- **Groups.** `house:<HouseId>`: the house's members are the keyframe's `house_member` rows of that house plus every person with a `LIFE_ENTRY` whose house walk (§4 rule 4, M7's rule) ends there; an event belongs to the group when a cast member is a member, **or when the event has no person in its cast and a told `triggered` edge leads to an event already of the group** (R5: an `INTERREGNUM_CRISIS` to the holder's `DEATH`, an `EXTINCTION` to the last `DEATH`). Castless events are decided in ascending `EventId` order, and a castless event that has joined counts for the later ones, so a chain of castless events joins in one pass (triggered edges point to earlier ids; R22). `line:<PersonId>`: the person and every descendant through life-course rows (channel A, then the keyframe), with the same edge rule. `year`: every event. One event may appear in two houses' annals (a marriage) and does, with the same sentence and sidecar row in each.
- **Order.** Events in `EventId` order (emission order, ADR-0025), which is tick order and, within an instant, phase order: a death at `Vital` precedes the succession it triggers at `Polity`. A year heading opens each calendar year that holds an event; the prologue (§6), when there is one, precedes under its own heading.
- **The text grammar, exactly (R1).** Line 1: `# The annals of <house name>` for a house (a house or a title with no name in the names file is refused by name: no `house #<id>`, no `title #<id>` reaches the text, R27), `# The annals of the line of <designation of the root>` for a line, `# The annals of the years` for the years. Line 2: `# From the chronicle of <world id>; templates <templates.version>`. Then, for a house with a prologue: a blank line, `## Before the chronicle began`, the prologue sentences one per line. Then, for each calendar year that holds a rendered event, in order: a blank line, `## The <ordinal> year (<epoch_label>)` (before the epoch, `## The <ordinal> year before the epoch (<epoch_label>)`), the year's sentences one per line. Every sentence is its template text with every slot substituted and the first character upper-cased; nothing else is changed. A heading line starts with `#`; a sentence line never does. The file ends with one LF.
- **The calendar.** A tick is minutes from the epoch (`05.1` §4.1). The day is `tick div 1440` (floor division, so a tick before the epoch falls in the day that contains it), the year `day div days_per_year`, the month from the month lengths (twelve 30-day months, or 31, 28, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31), no leap year. Year 0 is `the first year`; year −1 is `the first year before the epoch`. Ordinals and cardinals are English words everywhere, British style, up to 999,999 (`first`, `twelfth`, `thirteenth`, `twenty-first`, `one hundred and third`, `one thousand and first`, `two thousand and twenty-fourth`, `nine hundred and ninety-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-ninth`; `three living members`; `the second son`): one style (F23), because 1,000-year runs are routine; a larger number is refused by name (R25). Months have no names in the Registry, so dates are numeric: `day 14 of month 3`.
- **Left out, explicitly (R5, R15).** (i) An event of a kind outside the scope: counted in the sidecar trailer `# omitted: <n> event(s) of kinds outside the template set: <KIND> x<n>, ...`, the kinds in name order (R21). (ii) A castless event of an in-scope kind with no told `triggered` edge into the group: counted in `# castless: <n> event(s) with no cast and no told triggered edge into this group: ...`; never dropped silently. (iii) An event the chronicle does not hold: nothing in the annal depends on a refused draft except a cause slot, which then says "an event the chronicle does not tell" and records `untold/<id>`. **D38 either way:** under the admission rule every P1a world is under the ceiling, so every birth, marriage, death and succession is a row and v0 tells all of them; above the ceiling the chronicle itself omits commoner acts, the annal tells what the chronicle tells, and a named actor's acts stay (criterion 2). v0 adds no tellability or significance filter of its own. `05.4` §5 defines the Annals form as "templates over matches and significant events"; v0 renders every told event of its group, which at P1a scale is the same set, and selection by significance is P2.8's (R15). (iv) `STEP_DIGEST`, `KEYFRAME`, `INPUT` records: not events; ignored.

## 6. Opening a house whose past is only in the keyframe (the prologue)

PR #62 (SPR-019) back-fills a dynasty's pre-epoch genealogy and each title's past holders into the genesis keyframe: closed life-course rows with `exit_tick < 0` for the dead, `house_member` rows for every agnatic descendant of the root, tenure rows by the title's law. **No channel-A record exists for the back-filled dead** (REV-SPR-019 R1): the living founders have a `LIFE_ENTRY` at tick 0 whose `mother_id` and `father_id` point at persons the stream never mentions. So a house's past is readable only from the keyframe, and a house annal opens with a prologue rendered from the keyframe extract (§1; the Architect keeps the extract a plane-C input file bound by digest, R9, and keeps the prologue, R15 Q1):

| Prologue template | Sentence | Persons and sources (sidecar) |
|---|---|---|
| `prologue.house` | `House Ashcombe entered the chronicle with three living members.` (members of the house whose keyframe row has no exit) | persons: the living members; sources: `house/<id>`, `kf:life/<pid>` of each living member |

## 7. The lint (`lint_templates.py`)

Run on the real set in `check_all --part core` and on seventeen rendered controls, each giving exactly one diagnostic (`tests/lint_fixtures/README.md`; R29). It refuses, each by name:

1. a slot outside the grammar of §3 (`weather`, `state.title.holder`, `witness` in the singular), or a slot with a conversion or format spec: "is outside the envelope …", "carries a conversion or format spec";
2. a role slot the kind's `[[emits]] roles` does not list; `witnesses` on a kind without `witness`; `father` on a kind other than `BIRTH`;
3. a snapshot slot the kind's `snapshots` does not declare with that attribute and role; `of = "event"` or `witness`, undefined in v0;
4. a payload slot without a layout, a layout whose `system` is not the emitter, whose `cited` text is not the whole `Payload: [...]` clause of the emitter's `when`, or whose slots do not number the clause's items;
5. `motive` on a kind that declares no `motives`; a cause slot in a `label`; a `[[label]]` whose slots are not roles;
6. a kind in scope with no template, a template whose kind is out of scope, a kind whose last variant has a `when`, a kind with an unconditional variant that is not last, a duplicate template id, a non-integer version;
7. an out-of-scope kind any emitter declares that has no `[[label]]`; a `[[label]]` for an in-scope kind or an undeclared kind;
8. a `when` with an unknown key, a role the kind does not declare (`witness` included), a payload id not in the slot's enum, an edge kind outside the three, a `motive` key not `named` or `unnamed` or on a kind without motives;
9. an enum whose `ids` differ from the emitter's enum column codes (`column = "M2.demography:person.death_cause"`) or from the Registry list it names (`registry = "motive_edges"`), or that names neither; an enum whose `ids` and `words` differ in length; a missing prologue template, a prologue id outside the four, a prologue version that is not an integer, or a prologue slot outside that template's own vocabulary (R27); a control character in any text or label (R27); a `[[label]]` kind declared twice;

It reads `specs/systems/*.odd.toml` and `specs/systems/next/*.odd.toml` (a next version supersedes its frozen one by system id, the `_template` file skipped), so the templates follow the spec text the sprints build against; when the 0.3.0 M2 freezes, nothing here changes.

## 8. The sidecar and the output files

A group renders to two files, `<world>_<group>.txt` and `<world>_<group>.sidecar.txt` (`group` with `:` as `-`), UTF-8, LF.

**The text:** §5's grammar. Every sentence line has exactly one sidecar row, keyed by its 1-based line number, so a reader audits a sentence by its line.

**The sidecar:**

```text
#rehoboam/annals/v0 world=<id> group=<group> timeline=<u32> chronicle_digest=<xxh3-128 hex> registry_version=<dictionary> keyframe_extract=<xxh3-64 hex or -> state_hash=<the extract's genesis state_hash or -> manifest_hash=<sha256:64 hex or -> templates=<version> templates_digest=<xxh3-64 hex> renderer=annals_v0/0.3.0 calendar=<360|365>
P <line> <template id> <version> [] [person ids] [sources]                 # a prologue sentence
S <line> <template id> <version> [event ids] [person ids] [sources]        # a chronicle sentence
# omitted: <n> event(s) of kinds outside the template set: <KIND> x<n>, ...   # when any
# castless: <n> event(s) with no cast and no told triggered edge into this group: ...   # when any
digest <xxh3-64 hex>
```

The header names every input the render read (R9): the timeline, the chronicle's `canonical_digest` (for several segments, the fold of §9.1), its dictionary version, the keyframe extract's digest and the genesis `state_hash` it carries (`-` without an extract), the manifest hash behind the names file, the template set's version and digest (the XXH3-64 of `templates_v0.toml`'s bytes, printed as the integer's 16 hex digits like every `hash64` in the repository: R1), the renderer's version and the calendar. `event ids` are every event the sentence shows: its own, plus a cause slot's cause (V-NAR-05's "the event IDs it narrates"). `person ids` are every person the sentence designates (its roles, the persons its cause labels designate, and a birth's father), plus the parents a designation reaches (R21 (a)); a cited cause contributes the persons its label designates, not its whole cast. `sources` are the records read beyond the event: `name/<pid>`, `life/<pid>`, `kf:life/<pid>`, `kf:member/<pid>`, `kf:tenure/<title>/<start>`, `house/<id>`, `title/<id>`, `untold/<event>`. Ids sort numerically and sources bytewise, comma-joined, so a row is canonical. A sibling rank's sources are the life-course rows of every child of that parent of the same sex (R21 (b)). Ids are written in decimal in the sidecar and as 16 lowercase zero-padded hex digits wherever a prefix is taken (§4 rule 4).

## 9. Determinism, the digests and the manifest

- **The render is a pure function** of the chronicle file, the Registry, the calendar, the names file, the keyframe extract and the template file: no clock, no random draw, no environment, no iteration over a hash map that reaches the output (events are rendered in id order, members and sources are sorted). The reference renders every fixture group in two other interpreter processes under different `PYTHONHASHSEED` values and requires byte equality with the in-process render (check 6; R29), and recomputes every committed sidecar's digest from the files (`verify` mode, F26).
- **`annals_digest = XXH3-64("rehoboam/annals/v0" ‖ templates_digest ‖ text ‖ LF ‖ the sidecar's lines before its digest line)`**, with `templates_digest` as 8 little-endian bytes, the hash `rh_snapshot::hash::xxh3_64` and `canonical_v0.py` already share, printed as 16 hex digits. It is the last line of the sidecar. The same chronicle under the same template file gives the same digest on every machine; a changed template word changes every digest, which is the point.
- **`keyframe_extract`** is the XXH3-64 of the extract's canonical form, not of its file (R23): the line `extract <keyframe> <state_hash>`, then `house <id>` in id order, `title <id>` in id order, `life <person> <sex> <mother> <father> <birth_tick> <exit_tick|~> <founder|~>` in person order, `member <person> <house>` in person order and `tenure <title> <holder> <start> <end|~>` in (title, start) order, each line ASCII with one LF, hashed after the domain prefix `rehoboam/annals/keyframe-extract/v0` (the bytes of the format string, no line feed: R32). A shuffled or re-commented extract file gives the same digest (check 10).
- **`chronicle_digest`** is the chronicle's `canonical_digest` (`canonical_v0.py`, XXH3-128 as 32 hex digits) for a chronicle of one segment; for one of several, the XXH3-128 of the segments' 16-byte digests concatenated in segment order (R21). §9.1 states the file of several segments, its checks and the reference's `fold` (AN-0); the fixtures are files of one segment, and the renderer reads both forms.
- **The annals manifest** (R11), one per world: `<world>.manifest.txt`, listing the houses in id order, then `line:<the least named id>` when the world has houses, then `year` (R21). The reference also renders and checks groups the manifest does not list (Edric Ashcombe's line, `EXTRA_GROUPS`, so the line grouping stays covered now that the least named id is Orm's), and refuses a file in `golden/` that no rendered group or manifest owns as `STALE: orphan` (R39):

```text
#rehoboam/annals/manifest/v0 world=<id> chronicle_digest=<hex> templates=<version> templates_digest=<hex>
group <group> <annals_digest>        # the groups in (kind, numeric id) order: house:…, line:…, year
digest <xxh3-64 of the lines above, each with its LF>
```

  How a timeline's `narrative_hash` folds annals is P2.8's question (`05.11` §9); this contract fixes what there is to fold. `STEP_DIGEST`, a channel-A record the engine writes, never covers an offline artefact.

### 9.1 A chronicle of several segments: the `#segment` blocks and the fold (AN-0)

A run seals one channel-A segment per sim-year (`channel_a_v0.sql` R5), so a real run's chronicle has many segments, and `rh-cli annals-inputs` (SPR-029 T-01) writes them as one file. That file is **a canonical file** (`canonical_v0.md` §1: the header, the record lines in `seq` order, comment lines) in which one **segment line**, itself a comment, opens the records of each segment:

```text
#segment y<sim_year> first_seq=<u64> last_seq=<u64> canonical_digest=<32 hex>
```

`y<sim_year>` is the segment's sim-year in six zero-padded decimal digits, the name of its directory in the run (`y000012`, R5); `first_seq` and `last_seq` are the `seq` of its first and last record, as canonical ints (`canonical_v0.md` §2: no leading zero); `canonical_digest` is the segment's digest (`canonical_v0.md` §4), 16 bytes in canonical form, as 32 lowercase hex digits. The three values are the `segment` table's columns of the same names, so the exporter writes the line from the table and the reader checks it against the records. Tokens are separated by one space, and the line ends with LF like every line of the file. Because a segment line is a comment, `canonical_v0.py --digest` reads the file unchanged; its plain digest covers every record, and it is the chronicle digest only of a file of one segment. A **block** is a segment line and the record lines after it, up to the next segment line or the end of the file; other comment lines (`tail`'s `# STATUS …` and `#^ …`) are ignored wherever they stand.

The reference's `fold FILE`, and `load_chronicle`, which every render calls, read the file in this order and refuse it at the first rule it breaks, naming the line (`<file>:<line>:`) and the segment:

1. **The canonical file.** The whole file is checked first, as `canonical_v0.py --digest` checks it (ASCII, LF, the header, every record line, one timeline and channel, contiguous `seq`, non-decreasing `tick`), with that reference's own message. **Two blocks swapped** are refused here, at the first record whose `seq` no longer follows (`seq 0 does not follow 3`): contiguity fixes the order of the blocks, and the fold needs no rule of its own for it.
2. **No segment line:** the file is a chronicle of one segment, as every fixture is, and `chronicle_digest` is its `canonical_digest`, exactly as before AN-0. Nothing below applies.
3. **Line by line, in file order.** A line that begins with `#segment` matches the grammar above exactly, or it is refused as `not a segment line`: a five-digit year, a double space, a leading zero, an uppercase hex digit, a missing or an extra token, `#segments`. A near miss is never read as a free comment, which would silently merge two segments. A segment line's year is new and above every earlier one: a year already used is refused (`segment y000003 repeats (line N)`), a lower one too (`segment y000002 follows y000003: the years must increase`), so **segment lines swapped with their records left in place** are refused here. The years need not be consecutive: a sim-year with no record has no segment (`rh-stream`'s writer seals a year only when it holds a record; the `solitary` fixture cuts to y000000 and y000003). A record before the first segment line is refused (`a record before the first #segment line`): every record belongs to a segment, and an unnamed leading block would have no digest to check.
4. **Block by block, in file order.** A block holds at least one record, or it is refused (`segment y000001 holds no record`): a channel-A segment is never empty (`segment.record_count > 0`, and the writer skips a year with no record), and the empty string's digest, `99aa06d3014798d86001c324468d497f`, is a channel-C segment's, which no annal reads, so **an empty segment has no form**. Its `first_seq` and `last_seq` equal the `seq` of its first and last record, or the field is refused by name (`segment y000003: first_seq=2 on its #segment line, but its first record has seq 1`). Its `canonical_digest` is recomputed from its record lines (`canonical_v0.md` §4) and equals the segment line's, or the file is refused naming the segment, its records and both digests (`segment y000003 (records on lines 5-7): its #segment line says canonical_digest=…; its records recompute to …`).
5. **The fold.** With **one block**, `chronicle_digest` is that block's `canonical_digest`, which is the file's plain `canonical_digest`: §9's one-segment rule, so a file and its one-block form agree. With n ≥ 2 blocks, `chronicle_digest = XXH3-128(d_1 ‖ … ‖ d_n)` over the blocks' 16-byte digests in file order (which is `seq` order and year order), seed 0 and the default secret, printed in canonical form as 32 lowercase hex digits. The segment lines' years and `seq` ranges are not hashed: the ranges are inside the records the digests cover, and the year is a reader's label.

The fold reads no calendar, so it does not check a record's tick against its block's year; the exporter takes the year from the `segment` table it reads (T-01). `fold FILE` prints one line per block, `segment y<year> first_seq=… last_seq=… canonical_digest=…`, then `chronicle_digest=<32 hex>`; a refusal prints `FOLD REFUSED: <file>:<line>: …` and exits 1. `segment --world DIR [--out FILE]` writes a fixture directory's chronicle in this form, one block per sim-year that holds a record (a record's year is its `tick` divided by the calendar's minutes per year, floored), which is how check 11 and the `check_all` control make their cuts; no committed file is written in this form. A chronicle of several segments renders the same text and sidecar rows as the one-segment file of the same records; only the sidecar header's `chronicle_digest` (§8) and the manifest's (§9) carry the fold (check 11).
