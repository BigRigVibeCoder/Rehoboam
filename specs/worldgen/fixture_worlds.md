### 2.2 V-INV-12: great houses, naming, dormancy and re-promotion

**The naming rule, as M7 0.3.4 writes it (SIM-9).** A person is named at world start iff a cast member (none here) or a great-house member who meets M7's criteria, which are three clauses of the frozen spec: the `dormancy` submodel ("Yearly, and at world start"), under which a member is named when holding a seat, office or claim, or when within `dormancy_degrees` kinship steps of a holder of a title, claim or office of its house (over the life-course columns, through dead kin: the effect language's `kin(p, n)`), or when it is the ruling house's heir; the `on_birth` submodel, under which a newborn of a ruling house who is first or second in the line of one of its titles meets the criteria at once; and the Promote command's statement of the same ("a seat, office, claim or active vow, or within dormancy_degrees of a holder"). At genesis no offices, claims or vows exist (SPR-019 back-fills titles and tenures only), so the named at tick 0 are: the five holders; every living house member within `dormancy_degrees = 2` parent-or-child steps of the holder of its house's title; and the first and the second in each title's line (R2). The exact reference computes both the **narrow** reading (holders and the two-step neighbourhood) and the **full** one (plus the heir and the second in line), pins each title's line at genesis with its heir and second as `(k, kinship distance from the holder)` and its length, and the full reading is the gate's; beside them the living adults beyond two degrees who are not named under the full reading (dormant-eligible at the first pass, which runs at world start) and the living minors beyond two degrees who are not named (whether a minor is named at start is SPR-021's question: the criteria do not mention age, the vocation step does). A far adult heir meets both clauses at once, Demote's (an adult beyond `dormancy_degrees` with no seat) and Promote's (the ruling house's heir); the full reading resolves the overlap in favour of naming (the reference counts him as named and not as dormant-eligible), pending M7's own precedence between the two, which is **BCK-002 A-57**, for SPR-021 (R18, R26).

(`golden/fixture_worlds_v0.txt`; the golden's `dynasts` line counts every living back-filled person, the in-married wives included: 70, 106 and 87. The heir clauses add 2 to 4 named at each seed, the 2 to 7 the review measured.) Seed 1's House Ashcombe is the fixture's sharpest case: a 61-year-old lord whose children are dead; his heir under primogeniture is founder 8, a collateral five kinship steps away, and the second in line six steps away, so under the narrow reading the lord is the house's only named member and under the full reading three are named, while nine adult members (the heir excluded) are dormant-eligible from the first pass. When he dies the succession is immediate (M7 `on_death` arbitrates in-step, SAGA-13): founder 8 is seated, the two-step neighbourhood re-centres on him, and the members who were dormant-eligible around the old lord are, if within two steps of the new one, promoted at the next pass.

**Dormancy and re-promotion over 300 years.** M7's `dormancy` submodel runs yearly and at world start ("recompute claim.rank and send Demote and Promote by SIM-9's criteria"); T1 applies Promote and Demote (deferred while a break is pending). Three mechanisms move the named set, all simulation criteria (no narrative input):

1. **The seat moves.** Each succession re-centres the two-degree neighbourhood on the new holder: the old holder's distant cousins fall out, the new holder's children and siblings come in; the new heir and second are named at once. With tenures of 20 to 30 years (SPR-019's back-filled logs: 2 to 8 tenures per house over 3 to 6 generations), each house re-centres 10 to 15 times in 300 years.
2. **A house loses its seat.** When a title's line is empty under its law, M7 arbitrates among the pressed claimants and, for a title with neither, the liege's re-grant candidates; a sovereign title with no candidate at all is an `INTERREGNUM_CRISIS` (§2.3), and a regrant can seat another house. The former house, with no holder, office or claim, has no named member: a **dormant house**. It is re-promoted when a member gains a claim (`claim.rank` recomputed at the Year pulse: a descendant of a past holder within `holders_of(title, 16)` is in the line) or a seat (a regrant of a vacant title to eligible kin). The tanistry houses are where this happens: their lines at genesis are 1 to 5 persons long (adult males within three agnatic steps), and SPR-019's sweep measured the stand-in fallback (no living adult male in the house) in 6 of 55 tanistry successions on the manor, 25 of 264 at six generations, and 1 of 200 under primogeniture.
3. **A death or a birth.** `on_death` and `on_birth` send Promote for a newborn first or second in a line; a death of a named member removes one and may move the heir.

The gate's assertion ("ruling houses and active great-house members are T1 from world start; dormancy and re-promotion follow simulation criteria only; any person's genealogy reconstructs from the life-course log") is checkable on this world because the reference pins the named set at tick 0 under the rule as written and the Developer logs Promote and Demote with their reasons; the companion does not predict the number of dormancy transitions over 300 years (they depend on M7's succession draws and T1's claims), only that four of the five houses at seeds 1 and 2, and all five at seed 3, have dormant-eligible members at the first pass (House Carrow, the five- to nine-member tanistry house, has every living member within two steps of its chieftain at seeds 1 and 2) and that two of the five houses stand under the law whose line empties.

### 2.3 V-INV-03 and the contested succession: the arithmetic

**M2's hazard, on everyone.** At e0 30 the crude death rate of the stationary population is about 2.1% a year: the CRN reference's M2-only runs give 311 and 323 deaths in 10 years of 1,500 persons at seeds 1 and 3 (`specs/determinism/golden/crn_pairing_v0.txt`). Over 300 years, about 10,000 M2 deaths, among them most holders' (on the F1 schedule a holder's yearly q is 1.7% at 40, 2.7% at 50, 4.6% at 60 and 8.2% at 70).

**M7's ruler exits, on the two sovereigns** (M7 `exit_hazards`, the frozen 0.3.4): per sovereign per month, `h_m = murder_hazard_base × L × m_ec × (predecessor_killed_or if the predecessor was murdered)` and `h_d = deposition_hazard_ref × L × m_ec × (predecessor_deposed_hr if the predecessor was deposed or murdered)`, `L = primogeniture_hr` under primogeniture and 1 under tanistry, `m_ec = 1 + (exec_constraint_hr − 1) × exec_constraint = 1` with M6 absent, the monthly probability `h × D_m / D` as Prob32. The predecessor factors read the current ruler's `predecessor_exit` only: a ruler who follows a deposed ruler draws at 1.7 times the reference deposition hazard, and the ruler after that draws at the reference again unless the deposition repeated; the factors do not accumulate along a run of exits. At the manifest's values, per ruler-year at the reference state:

| Sovereign | L | Deposition `h_d` | Murder `h_m` | Expected in 300 ruler-years | After a deposed predecessor (deposition × 1.7) | After a murdered predecessor (deposition × 1.7, murder × 2.8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lord of Ash (primogeniture) | 0.3 | 0.009 | 0.003 | 2.7 depositions, 0.9 murders | 0.0153 / 0.003 | 0.0153 / 0.0084 |
| Chieftain (tanistry) | 1 | 0.03 | 0.01 | 9 depositions, 3 murders | 0.051 / 0.01 | 0.051 / 0.028 |

So the expected count of depositions across both realms in 300 years is 11.7 at the reference state (more after each deposed or murdered predecessor); the probability that **no** deposition happens anywhere in 300 years is e^−11.7 ≈ 8 × 10⁻⁶, and that no murder happens e^−3.9 ≈ 2%. The subordinate titles (Heir, Reeve, Tanist) draw no exit hazards (sovereigns only). A deposition is not a death: the deposed ruler lives, gains a claim and is excluded from the arbitration that follows his deposition (M7 `exit`), so the murder and the agenda routes, not the deposition, are V-INV-03's non-M2 death paths. The Developer measures the realised counts at seeds 1 to 3; the companion's expectation is the Poisson mean above, with the 300-year variance of the same size.

**The contested succession: the computable routes (R8).** M7's arbitration scores each candidate `arb_legal_weight × [legal heir] + arb_claim_weight × [pressed claim] + arb_military_weight × [holds the marshal office] + arb_treasury_weight × [holds the steward office]`, and a sovereign title is seated only when the leader's score exceeds the runner-up's by at least `contest_margin` (0.5) or the leader is alone; otherwise `INTERREGNUM_CRISIS`, `state = interregnum`, a resolution timer `interregnum_max_days` later, and a re-arbitration decided by `arbitration_tie` if still contested. Two routes reach it from this manifest:

1. **The empty line.** When the law's line is empty at a sovereign's death and no claim is pressed, the title has no candidate: `INTERREGNUM_CRISIS` by construction. The reference pins every title's line at genesis: `kingdom_10k` seed 2's High Fells sovereign (house `fell`, tanistry) has an **empty line at genesis** (`line 0`), so its first succession is a crisis unless a claim is pressed first; `twin_vales`'s tanistry lines are 1 to 5 long (carrow at seed 2: one candidate, no second), and a line of one or two persons empties with one or two deaths. Tanistry's line is the adult males within three agnatic steps of the holder, so it empties whenever the house's adult men are few, which SPR-019's sweep measured as the stand-in case (6 of 55 manor successions); and it refills when an agnate within three steps comes of age (`majority_age` 16), so an empty line at genesis is a window of some years, not a permanent state: the crisis route needs the sovereign to die inside it. Under primogeniture the line (the holder's descendants and the blood of the last 16 holders) is 7 to 22 long at genesis and empties only with a house.
2. **A pressed claim at a later succession.** A deposed ruler (or a passed-over heir, who gains a claim when someone else is seated) holds a claim; if pressed, it scores 0.6 against the legal heir's 1.0, a lead of 0.4 under `contest_margin`, so that succession is contested. The pressing is T1's agenda decision (`press_claim`, polled every 12 months by its considerations) and is not computable from the specs; the Developer counts `INTERREGNUM_CRISIS` events by route at seeds 1 to 3.

**The agenda action.** T1's `MURDER_ATTEMPT` (the `feud` decision, polled every 6 months) succeeds with `attempt_success 0.3` and submits `ScheduleDeath {cause = murder}` through M2's single death path (M7's `exit_murder` likewise). Its count is not computable from the specs: it depends on opinions, kin harmed and rival claims; the Developer reports it with the two above. V-INV-03's assertion is per death path, not per count: each produces the same consequence set (ledger removal, life-course exit, succession trigger, inheritance of titles and claims, the death event). If no agenda murder occurs on `twin_vales` in 300 years at seeds 1 to 3, the agenda path falls back to `kingdom_10k`'s 1,000-year G1a runs at seeds 1 to 3 (twelve courts, a thousand years: the agenda polls thousands of feud decisions), and if those show none either, the gate reports the gap rather than a pass (R20; T1's isolation court, round 1's suggestion, cannot observe V-INV-03's consequence set because M7 is stubbed there).

### 3.2 The named-actor rule and the bound (R2, R7)

The rule that promotes a named actor is M7's as written (§2.2): a member holding a seat, office or claim; a member within `dormancy_degrees` kinship steps of a holder of a title, claim or office of its house; the ruling house's heir; a newborn first or second in a title's line; plus the cast (empty here). The bound ≤ 500 is therefore the sum over holders of their two-step member neighbourhoods, plus each title's heir and second, plus claim holders, minus the overlaps. Kinship is counted in parent-or-child steps: within two steps of a holder are its parents, children, siblings (through a parent), grandparents, grandchildren and co-parents (a holder's wife is two steps away through a shared child); uncles, aunts, nieces and nephews are three steps and are not within reach at `dormancy_degrees = 2`. Only house members count: at genesis a holder's in-married wife and his mother are kin but not members (the back-fill writes no house_member row for a wife), so they are not named; after the saturation of §3.3 nearly everyone born in the world is a member, which adds the wife and the mother of each holder to his neighbourhood (about 2 per holder) and nothing beyond it, since the neighbourhood is bounded by the steps, not by membership.

**At genesis** the exact reference counts it: **65 named at seed 1, 71 at seed 2** under the full reading (58 and 67 under the narrow one), with 27 and 54 dormant-eligible adults and 25 and 41 minors beyond two degrees, the named excluded, among 117 and 166 living members (`golden/fixture_worlds_v0.txt`, per house there). The twelve holders are older than a mid-reign holder (36 to 74, most past 50, their children partly dead) and have 2 to 8 living kin within two steps each (members and in-married wives, a wife being two steps away through a shared child), mean 5.

**Over 1,000 years** the named count is not one the specs let one compute, but it is bounded by the rule: 12 holders (no office exists in P1a, and M7 creates no seat: "finite seats") × (1 + the two-step member neighbourhood, about 7 to 11 for a holder in his forties at e0 30 and TFR 4.9 once membership has saturated: 2 parents at most, a wife, 2 living siblings, 2 to 3 living children, 1 grandchild; a grandchild's other parent is three steps away and outside) + 12 heirs and 12 seconds (mostly already inside the neighbourhoods) + the claim holders (deposed rulers and passed-over heirs: a handful at a time). Expected range: **100 to 200 named** at any instant, far under 500. The lever, if the Developer's measurement contradicts this, is the manifest's `dormancy_degrees` (at 1 a neighbourhood is parents and children only); the reference pins the genesis count under 2 (the manifest's), and `--control dormancy-one` shows the count moving under 1. **SPR-030's G1a run asserts the yearly maximum of `person.named` ≤ 500** over the 1,000 years at seeds 1 to 3 (§6, R7); M7's own text says the count is reported and never enforced, so the manifest, not the engine, is what changes if it fails.

### 3.3 The population over the horizon, with the house-member mechanics (R1)

The commoner NRR alone does not give the trajectory, because three mechanics the manifests set act on great-house members and membership spreads: M7 `on_birth` writes a newborn's house_member row from the father's house, else the mother's (cognatic), so a child of a member and a commoner is a member; M2 F3/F4 multiply the fertility level of every woman with a house_member row by `elite_fertility_mult` (1.2); and M7 `vocation` sends a member reaching majority with no seat, office, claim or first place in a line into holy orders with probability `vocation_holy_orders` (0.2), which removes the member from marriage and conception (M2 N3/N4/F5). `specs/worldgen/project_population.py` is the derivation, pinned in `golden/population_projection_v0.txt` and checked by `bin/check_all.sh`:

- **Rates** from the M2 F1 widowhood chain (`specs/demography/selftest_m2_f1.py` check 5, at e0 30, the husband 3 years older): a commoner woman's NRR is **1.0341** (r = +0.114% a year), a member woman's at the elite level **1.2449**; the mean age of net maternity T = 29.4 years; survival to T on the schedule l(T) = 0.461.
- **The model**: two types of women, commoner (c) and member (e); per generation of T years, `W_e' = W_e (1 − v) NRR_e + W_c NRR_c h` and `W_c' = W_c NRR_c (1 − h)`, with v = 0.2 the holy-orders share and h = m (1 − v) / (m (1 − v) + 1 − m) the share of members among the marriageable men a commoner woman marries at random (m the member share); the population is proportional to W_e + W_c. The capped case multiplies every hazard by M2 F1 MR11's density term m_d = min(4, 1 + max(0, N/K − 1)) (slope 1), which under proportional hazards scales each NRR by l(T)^(m_d − 1).
- **Its limits**: generations are discrete (no age structure, no overlap); mating is random (M2 N4 bounds husbands to the settlement and excludes the named, both of which slow the spread for commoner women far from a seat); holy orders is a 20% removal of member women at majority (the draw excludes the first in a line and seat holders, a handful); the back-fill's age structure and widowhood beyond the chain's are ignored; the capped scaling is the proportional-hazards approximation at one age. The model says what the mechanics imply to within tens of per cent; the engine's run reports the truth.

What the numbers say: membership spreads from 1.2% (`kingdom_10k`) or 3.7% (`twin_vales`) to nearly everyone by year 300, because every mixed marriage makes members; while it spreads, the population grows at a rate between the commoner's +0.11% and the member's (1 − 0.2) × 1.2449 = 0.996 per generation, which is slightly negative; once saturated, the whole population reproduces at 0.996 per generation, −0.014% a year, and drifts down. The manifests' 1.2 and 0.2 therefore give a roughly stationary world (uncapped `kingdom_10k` peaks at 12,600 and ends near 11,400, within 26% of 10,000 throughout; capped, +4% at the peak, 10,415 at year 88, and −9% at year 1,000, 9,080), and it sits on a knife edge: the saturated member NRR is 0.8 × 1.2449 = 0.9959, a 0.4% margin that is inside the model's own error, so the engine's run may drift up or down by tens of per cent over 1,000 years without contradicting the model. The sensitivity the review ran is in the golden too: at 1.0 / 0.2 the saturated population collapses (about 110 persons at year 1,000: holy orders removes 20% of every generation with no fertility to offset it); at 1.2 / 0.0 it explodes (6.4 million at year 1,000); at 1.0 / 0.0 it grows at the commoner rate to 31,000. The fixtures keep 1.2 and 0.2 so that G1a exercises the elite read (R1c); that a world's long-run growth hangs on two elite parameters because membership saturates is M7's design question, filed as **BCK-002 A-56** (whether membership stays cognatic, becomes agnatic, or is bounded by degree from the line), to be decided before SPR-021's plan with D40's revisit.

**The G1a criterion is not reinterpreted (R1a).** The performance run is 1,000 years of a 10,000-person world. `kingdom_10k` sets `density_capacity: 10000` as **SPR-030's first C1 change** once the M2 F1 spec that carries the parameter (SPR-015/SPR-016, wave 5) is frozen and the Registry has it; the frozen Registry 0.2.0 does not (the validator refuses `density_capacity: 10000` today with "unknown parameter"), which is the only reason the manifest does not carry it now. `twin_vales` keeps the default (R9). With the capacity set, the model keeps the 10k world between +4% and −9% of 10,000 over the whole run, and the budget of `docs/08` §9 is read at 10,000 as written.
