# Population synthesis v0 and the dynastic back-fill: companion to `population_synthesis.toml` (SPR-019)

*Status: draft companion, round 2 (REV-SPR-019 R1–R8 applied; ADR-0033 §4: prose for reasoning only; where it differs from the TOML, the TOML is the spec). Normative text: `specs/worldgen/population_synthesis.toml` `[algorithm.population_synthesis]` P0–P12 and `[algorithm.dynastic_backfill]` B0–B11 (the algorithm only, R4); the amendments of the frozen M2 and M7 texts are `[[amends]]` records in `specs/systems/next/M2.demography.odd.toml` (0.3.0) and `specs/systems/next/M7.dynasty.odd.toml` (0.4.0, new), lint-checked and locked. Executable contract: `specs/worldgen/selftest_population_synthesis.py` with `specs/worldgen/golden/population_synthesis_v0.txt`. The decisions (§4) and the director note (§7) live here.*

> **BLUF:** At genesis `rh-worldgen` does two things beyond the manifest's cast and households. For the anonymous rest of a people it synthesises persons from the compiled M2 schedule: a stationary age-sex marginal (integerised by systematic allocation with a keyed offset), married couples by fixed-point iterative proportional fitting of a wife-band by husband-band table to the married counts that the F1 nuptiality chain implies (the chain in Q0.64, the shares in Q0.32, the table rounded to both marginals exactly by a bipartite flow), dependent children assigned to eligible mothers, households, and residence by S3's rule. For a dynastic world it first writes each dynasty's pre-epoch history: a spine of `generations` ancestors anchored on a youngest generation born in the twenty years before the epoch, their wives, their children by M2's own monthly conception draws, every death by the schedule's inverse draw, every younger son's marriage and children, and the title's tenure log from the root's majority to the genesis holder, **each past holder selected by the title's succession law as the effect language's programs state it** (checked against `effect_ref.py` at 263 successions over five laws and twenty seeds: no difference), each dead row appended closed. Every draw is keyed under `rh-worldgen`'s reserved id, every quantity an integer in a stated width (u128 suffices everywhere), and the exact Python reference pins hamlet and a manor fixture at two seeds each, plus the manor under all seven laws. Eleven decisions with confidences are in §4; one director item, D46, is in §7.

## 1. Why the algorithm is a TOML fold file and not this document

ADR-0033 §4 makes `.odd.toml` the spec and prose its companion, and SPR-018's `[algorithm.worldgen_lite]` set the precedent for genesis. But the three systems whose columns genesis writes are frozen (`M2.demography` 0.2.1 on `main` with its 0.3.0 draft in `specs/systems/next/`; `M7.dynasty` 0.3.2; `S3.migration` 0.1.0), so an `[algorithm.*]` table cannot land in any of them before SPR-019's own freeze. SPR-016 solved the same problem with a fold file that holds the tables and binds nothing until the Architect folds it. This spec does the same (`[fold]` names the targets), with one difference after round 1 (R4): the fold file keeps the algorithm only, and every change to a frozen sentence is a `[[amends]]` record in the next-version spec of its owner, where `lint_odd.py --next` checks the frozen text byte for byte and `amendments.lock.toml` pins the new one. `lint_odd.py` does not read `specs/worldgen`, so until the fold the exact reference and its gates are the contract; the reference reads the TOML and fails on a constant or check value that drifts (R5). **Recommendation: TOML fold file with this companion** (decision D-01, 8/10).

## 2. The pipeline, and what each step owes to the sources

| Step | What | Precedent (docs/14; research) |
|---|---|---|
| P2 | The stationary age-sex marginal from the compiled schedule's knots: cell `(a, s)` proportional to `l(aY) + l((a+1)Y)` times the sex ratio at birth, over the schedule's `A` years (120 at the defaults) | Lotka's stationary population (`c(a) ∝ l(a)` at `r = 0`); the schedule is `[algorithm.mortality_f1]`'s (Brass's General Standard at the manifest's `e0` and `beta`, the infant sub-schedule, the Perks tail; R12 §9); V-SF-01's band for `r` straddles 0 |
| P3 | Systematic allocation with a keyed offset over integer weights: each cell its floor or ceiling, totals exact, every product below 2^96 | humanleague's *integer* microsynthesis (docs/14 §C, DEPEND/PORT candidate) uses quasirandom sampling for the same reason: largest remainder on many small cells piles the units into the largest ones (decision D-10 records the failure on hamlet) |
| P5 | Married counts by age from the F1 nuptiality chain in Q0.64, rounded once to Q0.32, with a widowhood factor from the schedule | `[algorithm.partnering_f1]` N1 (the early, near-universal pattern, Hajnal 1965 as R09 §2 B summarises it); the husband three years older is the F1 reference's own chain; the chain's 419 roundings stay below Q0.32's resolution, so E(a) is the exact chain rounded once (check 4) |
| P6 | Iterative proportional fitting of the couple table to the two married marginals, seed = N2's preferred age gap | humanleague and ipfn (docs/14 §C: PORT of the idea, MIT); the arithmetic is ours: fixed point at 32 fraction bits, a fixed sweep count |
| P7 | Exact two-marginal rounding by a unit-capacity bipartite flow | the integrality of bipartite flows (a textbook property); PopulationSim's and humanleague's integerisation keep marginals the same way (docs/14 §C) |
| P9–P10 | Children allocated over mother bands by the model's own expectation (`n(a)` of Coale–Trussell 1974 times the child's survivorship), assigned to eligible mothers at least `sibling_gap_minutes` apart | `[algorithm.fertility_f1]` F1, F5 (the closed period `G + P` = 20 months; the gap is 609 days, at least M2's least birth-to-birth gap on either calendar: 602 on 360 days, 609 on 365; R10) |
| B3 | Deaths by inverse transform on the schedule's knots, conditional on survival to a known tick | `[algorithm.mortality_f1]` MR9 (the same `l`), so the ancestors died as M2 would have killed them |
| B4–B8 | The spine anchored at the epoch, M2's conception draws forward, younger sons' marriages | `[algorithm.fertility_f1]` F4–F6 (the Prob32 conception table), N2 (ages); the effect language's deep-world fixture (House Vey) is the shape the blood rule needs (PR #41 §5.3) |
| B9 | Tenures by the title's law: the effect language's succession protocol (§7.2) and programs (§8) restated over the back-filled rows at each holder's death; the court laws write the genesis row only | `specs/registry/effect_language_v0.md` §5.3, §7.2, §8 and `effect_ref.py` (PR #41); `M7.dynasty` 0.3.2 `arbitrate`; REV-SPR-019 R3 |

**Not reused, and why.** humanleague and ipfn are float libraries: a genesis table must be bit-identical across machines (V-INV-10), so the IPF is restated in fixed point and the integerisation over integer weights. synthpops (households, contact layers) is unmaintained and built for epidemic contact structure, not a dynastic substrate; its household-composition idea survives as P9's seed. The effect language's evaluator is not called by the reference (it is Python in the test tier, not the engine); B9 restates the programs so that `rh-worldgen` can implement them in Rust, and the differential gate keeps the restatement honest (§3).

## 3. Determinism rules the algorithm obeys

- **Keyed draws only** (`05.1` §6 item 2): every draw is `H(world_seed, canonical salt, system_id("rh-worldgen"), epoch 0, purpose, entity, t = 0, k)` under the reserved id of SPR-011 ruling 10. Six new purposes (`[[rng_purposes]]`): `synth_offset`, `backfill_spine`, `backfill_marriage`, `backfill_conception`, `backfill_sex`, `backfill_death`; `founder_birthday` and `founder_residence` are reused unchanged. Round 1's `backfill_sortition` is gone with the court laws' past holders (R3). Each purpose names its entity and its `k`; no stream, no rejection loop.
- **Fixed draw structure:** one offset draw per allocation, keyed by people index and slot; four spine draws per ancestor generation plus one; four per younger son; one death draw per person; per married pulse one conception draw and, on a hit, one day draw; one sex draw per child. A count depends only on the stated loops (the months a couple is married and fertile before the epoch), never on a retry.
- **No HashMap iteration, no float, no bignum:** cells are visited in index order, persons in ascending `PersonId`, candidates in the protocol's key order with `PersonId` as the final tie-break; the IPF is integer arithmetic at 32 fraction bits with one `rhe` per cell per step; the nuptiality chain is Q0.64 with one `rhe` per month; the shares are Q0.32; the allocations run on integer weights with the precondition `sum(w) × (total + 1) < 2^96`, which every caller meets at the manifest's population bound, and the ceiling is taken in the unsigned form `ceil(x − φ) = ceil(x + 1 − φ) − 1` (R15), so u128 suffices everywhere (round 1's exact rationals needed 13,000 bits: MAJ-2). The reference uses Python integers only (`fractions` appears once, in the check that E(a) equals the births reference's exact chain rounded once).
- **Every tie broken:** the systematic offset decides every rounding; the flow's BFS order is fixed; the laws' keys end in `PersonId`; assignment ties go to the lower id.
- **Nothing after the epoch:** every back-filled birth and death tick is a day start before tick 0; a pregnancy that would deliver at or after tick 0 is not carried (D-08); no timer, no event and no channel-A record exists at genesis for the back-filled dead (R1).
- **Genesis order:** the cast, the manifest households' children and servants (ruling 10), then the back-filled rows in creation order (houses in manifest order, each in full), then the synthesised cells; `founder(seed, k)` follows that order, so hamlet's existing founders keep their ids.
- **The differential gate (R3):** `selftest_population_synthesis.py --effect-ref DIR` rebuilds, at every pre-epoch succession of the five genealogical laws over the twenty sweep seeds, the back-filled log as an effect-language fixture world (`life_course`, `person` for the living, `title`, `tenure`, `M7.dynasty` params) and runs the law's program through `effect_ref.py`'s `succession_line`; the whole line must equal the reference's, not just its head. Run against the PR #41 branch copy: 263 successions, no difference. It joins `check_all --part core` when PR #41 merges and `specs/registry/effect_ref.py` exists on `main`; until then the spec says so (`[fold]`).
