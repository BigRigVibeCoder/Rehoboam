## 0. What this contract fixes, in one paragraph

An event is what a system emitted at the level merge: its kind, its **place, magnitude and secrecy** (set by the draft), its **origin** (stamped by the engine from the job that produced it), its **cast** (role rows: who played which Registry role), its **anchored snapshots** (the attribute values the emitter read at emission, keyed by role entity), its **typed causal edges** (each to an earlier event, with a kind from DATA-2, a motive from the Registry `[motive_edges]` when the kind is `motivated_by`, and a fixed-point weight and evidence), and its integer payload. Everything in it is plane A, enters the running event-log hash and therefore `state_hash`, and is written to channel A exactly as emitted. Nothing perceptual is in it (ADR-0035 §6, §7a): time of day, lighting, acoustics, inferred emotion, prop status and phrasing are derived in plane C from these fields (`05.11` §3 stage 3). A pattern's abstract role (`authority`, `kin`, …) is an **anchored attribute read**, filled as a snapshot like any other (§1, R1). Damage and transformation state carries its cause `EventId` inside the value, set through a buffered slot the merge rewrites (SAGA-15; §7, R2), and "what last happened here?" is a plane-B index over the chronicle (§7). One role vocabulary serves the chronicle, the stream, the Registry, the system specs and the sifting patterns (§1).

## 1. The role vocabulary (the reconciliation)

**Ruling asked of the Architect, recommended at 8/10; the reviewer found it sound.** The one vocabulary is the Registry `[event_roles]`, in list order: `actor` 0, `target` 1, `witness` 2, `instrument` 3. It is what channel A v0 already writes (`event_role.role`), what SPR-011 ruling 11 fixed for `DEATH` (the deceased is `target`), what the births spec fills (`BIRTH`: `actor` the mother, `target` the child; `MARRIAGE`: `actor` the husband, `target` the wife; the father stays outside the cast, in the life-course row, and a `parent` role is appended only if SPR-029 needs it: R12 (b)) and what `05.2` §4 names. `schema.sql`'s `concrete_role` (initiator, target, partner, bystander, instrument) and `abstract_role` go:

| Old | New | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `initiator` (0) | `actor` (0) | The same thing under the Registry's name. The pattern clause key `initiator` becomes `actor` (`specs/sifting/lint_patterns.py`, `patterns.example.toml`, `05.4` §2.2): clause keys are the `[event_roles]` ids, so a pattern binds a role variable to a cast member by the role's own name |
| `target` (1) | `target` (1) | Unchanged |
| `partner` (2) | dropped | A symmetric act names its two parties `actor` and `target` by a convention its verb states (`MARRIAGE`: husband, wife; `DESPAIR_PACT`: the breaking actor, the relation). No P1a verb needs a third party role; one that does (a mediated pact) appends a role to `[event_roles]` then |
| `bystander` (3) | `witness` (2) | The same thing: present, neither acting nor acted on. The knowledge rows of P1b (`knowledge.channel = witness`) are derived from these rows |
| `instrument` (4) | `instrument` (3) | Unchanged in meaning: the S6 artifact used. Its entity kind is `rh_ids::EntityKind::Artifact`, tag 5, **reserved now** in channel A's and the canonical form's `entity_kind` domain (0–5) and added to `rh-ids` with S6's sprint (R12 (c)); until then no event fills it |
| `abstract_role` | **dropped from plane A; the domain is an anchored attribute** (R1) | An abstract role (`authority`, `successor`, `kin`, `rival`, …; Registry `[roles]`) is the *domain* of a pattern's role variable (`roles = { A = "authority" }`, `05.4` §2.1). A domain is state with a time, and titles change *within* an instant (a `DEPOSED`, then a `SUCCEED`, in one tick), so reading it from `state_interval` at the event's tick would be an unanchored read: the deposed ruler would never be `authority` at his own deposition (REV-SPR-020 MAJ-1). The domain is therefore a declared **bool attribute** in `[attributes]`, captured as a snapshot at emission like `stress`: the Registry `[role_domains]` table maps each `[roles]` id to its attribute, the system that owns the source column and the column (`owner`, `column`: `authority` → `holds_title(actor)` from M7's `title.holder`; `kin` → `is_self_or_kin(actor, partner)` from the life-course log; R16 split the earlier `source` into the two, since any emitter that reads the column may declare the snapshot), and the pattern DSL keeps its surface, compiling `A = "authority"` to the anchored read `holds_title(A)@e1 == true` at **`A`'s anchor: the event of the first clause, in pattern order, whose cast binds `A` through `actor`, `target` or `instrument`** (a witness is many, so a witness-bound variable has no snapshot; a `match_event` clause has no cast, so a consumer of a match takes the emitter's bindings as the emitter checked them at its own anchor), and `lint_patterns.py` refuses a pattern with a domain on a variable that no clause so binds (R16; the example pack's `false_positive_purge`, `famine_to_succession_crisis` and `guest_right_violated` were corrected to it). A relational domain names its partner variable: `S = "successor of A"` compiles to `is_successor(S, A)@e1 == true`. A domain whose source does not exist yet (`investigator`, `suspect`, `host`, `guest`, `rival`, `ally`, `subordinate`, `member`: `owner = "none"`) is absent until its system declares the snapshot, and `lint_patterns.py` warns on a pattern that uses one. SIFT-1 holds as written; no requirement changes, so the earlier D45 is withdrawn (R1) |

What this changes on disk and in code: `schema.sql`'s `event_role` becomes channel A's child table column for column (`ordinal, entity_kind, entity_id, role`); the chronicle self-test's `ttb_*` views read `role = 0` and `role = 1`; `specs/stream/selftest_channel_a.py` checks that the three child tables of channel A equal `schema.sql`'s column for column (as it already does for `life_course`); `lint_patterns.py` reads the clause keys from `[event_roles]` and the domains from `[role_domains]`. Channel A, the canonical form and the `life_ends` twin do not change for roles.

**Multiplicity.** A verb's `[[emits]]` entry lists the roles it may fill (§3). Each listed role appears at most once on an event, except `witness`, which may repeat. An event may fill none (`EXTINCTION`, `INTERREGNUM_CRISIS`: a title is not an entity; the payload names it). A role not listed for the kind is `SimError::UndeclaredRole` at `StepCtx::emit`; a second row of a non-`witness` role is `SimError::DuplicateRole`; a cast of more than 256 rows is `SimError::CastTooLarge` (the ordinal is a `u8`; R12 NIT-5). A pattern clause with `witness = "W"` binds `W` to each witness row in turn: one partial per witness, as one `DEATH` with two targets gives two `life_ends` matches (`canonical_v0.md` §6).

**Canonical row order (the `ordinal`).** Role rows are ordered by role code, then by entity (`entity_kind`, then `entity_id` ascending); `StepCtx::emit` sorts the draft's rows into that order and refuses a duplicate `(role, entity)`. The ordinal is therefore a function of the set, so two implementations of one spec write the same rows and the same canonical line, and the canonical parser refuses a cast out of that order, a repeated row, two rows of a non-`witness` role, and more than 256 rows.

## 2. The `EventDraft` contract

`rh-events` (plane A, machinery) replaces SPR-006's `subjects` and untyped `causes` with the envelope. The shape, as the sprint builds it:

```rust
pub struct EventDraft {
    pub kind: &'static str,                 // the [[emits]] kind, a Registry verb
    pub place: Option<PlaceId>,             // event.place_id: the draft sets it (None until places exist, SPR-018)
    pub magnitude: i32,                     // event.magnitude: the draft sets it (0 unless the verb's spec gives it a meaning)
    pub secrecy: Secrecy,                   // event.secrecy: the draft sets it (0 public, 1 witnessed, 2 faction, 3 secret; public unless the spec says)
    pub roles: Vec<RoleRow>,                // RoleRow { role: RoleId, entity: EntityRef }
    pub snapshots: Vec<Snapshot>,           // Snapshot { attr: &'static str, entity: EntityRef, partner: Option<EntityRef>, value: i32 }
    pub causes: Vec<CausalEdge>,            // CausalEdge { cause: EventRef, kind: EdgeKind, motive: Option<&'static str>, weight: Q8_8, evidence: Q8_8 }
    pub payload: Vec<i64>,                  // as today: meaning per verb
}
// Builders: .at(PlaceId) .magnitude(i32) .secrecy(Secrecy)
//           .role(RoleId, EntityRef) .witness(EntityRef) .snapshot(ATTR, EntityRef, i32)
//           .relational_snapshot(ATTR, EntityRef, EntityRef, i32) .event_snapshot(ATTR, i32)
//           .caused_by(EventRef, EdgeKind) .motivated_by(EventRef, MOTIVE, Q8_8 weight, Q8_8 evidence)
//           .after(DraftRef) == .caused_by(EventRef::Draft(r), EdgeKind::Triggered) .with_payload(Vec<i64>)
// Not on the draft: origin (the engine stamps it, below), tick, phase, id, admitted.
```

- **Who sets each event-level field (R3, R13, R14).** `place`, `magnitude` and `secrecy` are the draft's: the emitter sets them from its own state (a `DEATH` at the deceased's residence once S3 tracks it; `secrecy` from the verb's spec, `public` by default). `origin` is the engine's: `StepCtx::emit` stamps every draft with the **job's origin**, and a system cannot set it. A job's origin is `sim` (0), except that the `Inputs`-phase job that applies a material input has that input's origin, and **origin propagates through every hop** (R14): every timer, command and draft a job creates inherits the job's origin (`Timer.origin`, `QueuedCommand.origin`), a timer's fire is a job of the timer's origin, and **a drain runs everything it does for one command under that command's origin** (R24): `ctx.under(&command, |ctx| { … })` scopes `emit`, `submit`, `schedule` and `set_cause` to the command's origin (`ctx.emit_from(&command, draft)` is the one-draft form), so the smoke X4 drain's `Audit` for an `oracle` `Settle` is `oracle`, and the timer a drain inserts for a command is too; an effect of several commands at once (X7's one `TRADE` over audits of mixed origins) is the job's, `sim`. So an Oracle input that re-samples T1's break timer gives the fire's `MURDER_ATTEMPT` the origin `oracle`, and so are the `DEATH_DUE` timer that fire inserts, the `ScheduleDeath` that timer submits and the `DEATH` M2 drains: `05.2` §4.0 criterion 4's plumbing ("carried through timers and lifecycle commands"), stated here as §4.0 defers it, and in `05.1` §2 rule 4 (v1.13); `selftest_envelope.py` check 7 models a three-hop chain per input and `--control origin-lost` must fail it. **The queued input carries it too** (R23): `MaterialInput` gains `origin` (the `Inputs` job reads it there, and a keyframe resume restores queued inputs from the keyframe, not from the journal), so `rh-core/engine.inputs` holds it as well. **Hash-neutral at `sim`, exactly** (R13, R24): the three rows gain optional **tails**, each a `u8 1` presence marker followed by the value, written only when the value is present or non-zero and decoded by the row's remaining length (rows are length-delimited in the plane-hash layout and the keyframe stores them as the same bytes, so the tail bytes are the keyframe's too): `rh-core/agenda.timers` is `i64 time, u8 phase, entity, u64 seq, u32 owner, u64 purpose, u32 kind, i64 payload, u8 tombstoned [, u8 1 ‖ u64 cause] [, u8 1 ‖ u8 origin]` (EVO-001 R15's `cause` tail first, when the timer carries one; then the origin tail when it is not `sim`: a remaining length of 0, 2, 9 or 11 bytes says which are present); `rh-core/engine.commands` is `str owner, i64 tick, u8 phase, u64 person, u64 seq, str sender, opt(u64 cause), str kind, list(i64 args), opt(u64 draft) [, u8 1 ‖ u8 origin]`; `rh-core/engine.inputs` is `i64 time, u32 index, list(entity scope) [, u8 1 ‖ u8 origin]`. Every timer, command and input that exists today is `sim` and hashes as before; the SPR-007 hash golden stays v1 and `gen_hash_golden.py` gains the tails as cases. **Which origin an input has** is a function of its journal kind (`05.9` IF-2 `InputKind`; stated on `schema.sql`'s `input_journal.kind`), never of its producer: `OracleMove`, `OracleWhisper`, `OraclePierce`, `OracleOmen`, `OracleWard` → `oracle` (3); `PlayerAction`, `SpeechAct` → `player` (1); `ForkOverride` → `director` (4). **Materiality is the journal's own rule** (`Scope::is_material`: an input is material exactly when it has a scope, R26), and this list follows it: `ManifestSet`, `SettingChange` and `ConsoleMode` carry no scope, so they are never material and reach no plane-A job; the journal's `append` refuses a scope on them (the sprint adds the refusal), since no origin exists for them. The smoke world's synthetic inputs are `OracleMove` rows with `Producer::System`, so they are `oracle` (§6). The `tick` and `phase` are the merge's, as today; the `id` and the admission flag (§6) too.
- **Names and codes.** A snapshot's `attr` and an edge's `motive` are the Registry **names** (`&'static str` constants the skeleton emits: `ATTR_STRESS`, `MOTIVE_GRIEF_FOR`), so the digest hashes `h(name)` and a Registry addition that sorts earlier moves no hash (R4). `RoleId(u8)` is the index in `[event_roles].ids`, pinned by the loader (§8). The writer maps names to the segment's dictionary indexes (`attr_id`, `motive`; §5 of `channel_a_v0.sql`). `bin/gen_skeletons.py` emits the constants from the Registry (or the smoke overlay) at generation time, so no system crate types a name; registration compares the skeleton's constants with the Registry it runs under, as it compares `SCHEDULE`.
- **`EventRef`** is EVO-001's: `Draft(DraftRef)` for one of the step's own earlier drafts, `Known(EventId)` for an id read from state (a timer's or command's `cause`, `feud.cause_event`, `artifact.chain_head`). `None` is not an edge. The merge replaces every `Draft` by the id it assigns in the same merge (`05.1` §2 rule 4), coalesces edges to one cause (§5), then sorts the edges by cause id; `EventLog::append` refuses a draft that still holds a `Draft`, a cause at or above the new id (`ForwardCause`, unchanged), a `motive` on an edge whose kind is not `motivated_by`, a non-zero `weight` off `motivated_by`, an `evidence` other than 1 on a deterministic kind (§5), and two edges to one cause.
- **`StepCtx::emit` checks the declaration** (the spec table at registration, as `emits_kind` does today): the kind is declared; every role is in the kind's `roles`, once (witness excepted), at most 256 rows; every snapshot's `attr` is one of the kind's `snapshots`, its entity is a cast member of the draft (or the event itself, §4), its value is in the attribute's domain; **every declared snapshot whose role is filled and whose source is present is on the draft** (`SimError::MissingSnapshot`; R10); every motive is one of the kind's `motives`. Each refusal is a `SimError` naming the system, the kind and the offending name or code.
- **What the merge does and does not do.** It assigns the id, tick and phase, resolves `Draft` refs in edges, in a timer's and a command's `cause`, in event-typed payload fields (EVO-001) **and in the buffered `set_cause` slots of §7**, coalesces and sorts edges, and computes the admission flag (§6). It adds no role, no snapshot and no edge: the `player`, `oracle` and `director` edge kinds have no writer in P1a (§5), so §2 and §5 agree (R3).

**How a system declares what it fills** (`specs/systems/*.odd.toml`, `[[emits]]`; `lint_odd.py` checks it against the Registry and the spec's own `[[owns]]` and `[[reads]]`):

```toml
[[emits]]
kind = "MENTAL_BREAK"
when = "..."
roles = ["actor"]                                            # [event_roles] ids this kind may fill; witness may repeat on an event
motives = ["GRIEF_FOR", "RETALIATION_FOR", "AMBITION_FOR", "JEALOUSY_OF", "VOW_OF"]   # [motive_edges] ids its motivated_by edges may carry
snapshots = [{ attr = "stress", of = "actor", from = "mind.stress" }]   # [attributes] id, the role (or "event") it is read of, the column it is read from
```

`roles` is required on a frozen spec (an error when missing; a warning on a draft: R7) and lists each role once; `of` is a role of the kind other than `witness` (a witness is many), or `event`; `motives` and `snapshots` default to none, and a repeated motive or snapshot key is an error. A wrong value (a role, motive or attribute the Registry lacks; a snapshot of a role the kind does not fill; a `from` the spec neither owns nor reads; a type that does not match, for an owned column, a foreign read column or an `algorithm.*` source, which declares its `type`; an enum source whose codes differ from the attribute's values, own or foreign) is an error on every spec.

## 4. The anchored snapshot rule

DATA-3: an event captures the predicate values that rules and patterns read **at emission**; `05.4` §2.1: a pattern reads them as `attr(args)@e`, the value captured in `event_snapshot` for the event bound to `e`; V-DATA-08 compares the stored value with the value at emission. The rule, exactly:

1. **Which attributes.** Exactly the `snapshots` the emitter's `[[emits]]` entry declares for the kind, no more and no fewer: `emit` refuses an undeclared snapshot and a missing declared one whose role is filled and whose source is present (R10); a declared snapshot whose role is unfilled on this event, or whose source is absent (rule 4), is absent. An attribute not declared for a kind is never on its events, so a pattern read `attr(X)@eN` is satisfiable only when every verb the clause `eN` can match declares `attr` with a role that binds `X`; `lint_patterns.py` gains that check when the first multi-clause pattern lands (P2.1), with the declarations in hand.
2. **Read when, by whom: the pre-act value** (R10). The emitter fills each snapshot from its own `SystemView` in the same step, with **the value the rule read before the act's own effects were applied**: for an own column, the value before the step writes it for this act (a `MENTAL_BREAK` snapshots the stress that crossed the threshold, not the stress after the relief); for a foreign column, the value the emitter's declared read returns under its declared lag (`05.1` §2 rule 4). So the snapshot is what the rule read, which is DATA-3's sentence, and V-DATA-08's oracle is the emitter's pre-act view, not the end-of-instant column. The engine never fills a snapshot.
3. **Of whom.** `of` names one of the kind's roles other than `witness` (the attribute's first argument is that role's entity) or `event` (the attribute's argument is the event itself: `weather`, `contaminants`, `cell_x`, `cell_y`); a two-argument attribute (`trust(actor, partner)`) names `partner` as a second role. An `event`-argument row stores `entity_kind 0, entity_id 0` (the world; "this event"). An attribute whose argument is *another* event (`knows_event(actor, event)`, `in_realm(event, actor)`) is declared in the Registry but not capturable until the knowledge tables exist (P1b), which fix its encoding then.
4. **Only what is simulated** (`05.2` §4.1a). A snapshot is written only when its source column's owner is registered: registration marks each declared snapshot whose `from` is a dropped read of an absent owner (`05.1` §2 rule 2, absent owners), and `StepCtx::emit` discards such a row silently, counted under `dropped{system, snapshot, absent_owner}`; the neutral value of a read is never a snapshot. No weather before M3, no emotion before P4 (T1 0.3.x: "no emotion snapshot is stored until then"), no guess in plane A.
5. **Encodings** (`event_snapshot.value INTEGER`, i32; the attribute's Registry `type` decides): `q8_8` the raw bits (value / 256: a value in [−128, 128), raw bits in [−32768, 32767]); `bool` 0 or 1; `enum` the index in the attribute's `values`; `int` the integer itself, refused at `emit` outside i32 (`contaminants` is the `[contaminants]` bitset in list order, bit *i* = `ids[i]`; `cell_x`, `cell_y` are cell coordinates). The source column's type must match: `q8_8` ↔ `q8_8`; `bool` ↔ `bool`; `enum` ↔ an `enum` column with the same `values` in the same order; `int` ↔ `u8`, `u16`, `u32`, `i32` or `i64` (the lint checks the pairing for an owned column, a foreign read column and an `algorithm.*` source alike; the i32 range at emit).
6. **Canonical order.** Snapshot rows are ordered by `(attr_id, entity_kind, entity_id, partner_kind, partner_id)`, a row without a partner before any with one for the same attribute and entity; `emit` sorts and refuses a duplicate key. The canonical line's `snapshots` list follows the same order.
7. **Domains are snapshots** (R1). `holds_title(actor)` on M7's events, `is_self_or_kin(actor, partner)` on an act between kin, and the other `[role_domains]` attributes are declared and filled exactly like `stress`, by the system that owns the source; a pattern's `roles = { A = "authority" }` reads them anchored (§1).
8. **In P1a** the only declared snapshot is T1's `stress(actor)` on its seven acts (table of §3); M2 and M7 declare none (M7's `holds_title` comes with its next version). `solo`, `household` and `hamlet` therefore write no `event_snapshot` row.

## 5. Causal edges: kinds, motives, weight, evidence

**Kinds.** `causal_edge.kind` keeps `schema.sql`'s codes, which channel A v0 and the canonical form fixed: 0 `contingent`, 1 `triggered`, 2 `relayed_knowledge`, 3 `motivated_by`, 4 `enabled`, 5 `probabilistic`, 6 `player`, 7 `oracle`, 8 `director`. Their use:

| Kind | Who writes it | When |
|---|---|---|
| `contingent` | a system whose verb declares `references` (Registry `[verbs]`) | only from an explicit unlocking reference (R02 §R2; V-DATA-09): the event names a past event it was enabled by (a `REVENGE` naming the harm; an `ARTIFACT_*` naming `chain_head`) |
| `triggered` | any system | the mechanical consequence of an earlier event in the same causal chain: a timer's or command's `cause` (EVO-001), a draft-local `after`, the death or deposition behind a succession |
| `relayed_knowledge` | the knowledge systems (P1b) | the event was possible because of a testimony hop; `evidence` is the fidelity of the hop |
| `motivated_by` | a system with a mind (`T1.minds`) | a utility decision's contributors (`05.2` §4: the top contributors and the margin), one edge per contributor before coalescing (below); with a `motive` when the Registry names the kind of motive (§3) |
| `enabled`, `probabilistic` | any system | an enabling condition that is itself an event (a famine behind a riot), or a hazard whose draw this event raised (`evidence` the share of the hazard the cause accounts for) |
| `player`, `oracle`, `director` | **no writer in P1a** | reserved for the event an input itself gets when acting in the past becomes a fork (P2.9, `05.9`): today an input is an `INPUT` record, not an event, and its consequences carry its `origin` (§2) instead. A system never writes these kinds; `append` refuses them until that format exists |

**DATA-2 lists eight kinds and the schema nine** (`oracle`, added with the `oracle` origin on 2026-10-15). V-DATA-09 says "only the eight kinds". This spec keeps the nine codes, because channel A v0 and the canonical form already refuse a kind outside 0–8 and an oracle input is a distinct origin everywhere else; the requirement text is the director's: **D44** (§10).

**Motives.** A motive edge is a `motivated_by` edge whose `motive` names a Registry `[motive_edges]` id (the name in the draft and the digest; the index in list order in a segment: `RETALIATION_FOR` 0 … `CORRUPTION_BY` 8). `motive` is NULL on every other kind and on a `motivated_by` edge whose contributor is not one of the named motives (an opinion term, a trait term). A kind code is never allocated per motive: the list grows by appending to the Registry, and segments carry its dictionary. V-SAGA-15's "every motive edge points to an earlier event" holds by construction (`ForwardCause`).

**Weight and evidence, fixed point.** Both are `Q8_8` raw bits in an i16 (`SMALLINT`): `weight` is signed, the contributor's contribution to the chosen option's score on T1's utility scale (an inhibiting contributor is negative), and **0 on every kind but `motivated_by`**; `evidence` is in [0, 1] (0 to 256): **exactly 1 (256) on a deterministic kind** (`contingent`, `triggered`, `player`, `oracle`, `director`), the hop's fidelity for `relayed_knowledge`, the attributed share for `enabled` and `probabilistic`, and the decision **margin** for `motivated_by` (how far the chosen option led the runner-up, saturated at 1; SPR-024 states the formula with the IAUS). `append` and the canonical parser refuse the other values (R8). Neither is ever a float (R2 of channel A).

**One edge per cause, by coalescing** (R9; total by R21). The key is `(effect_id, cause_id)`. A draft may carry several contributors to one cause (`GRIEF_FOR` and `RETALIATION_FOR` for the same death; a `triggered` link and a motive to one event); the merge coalesces them into one edge by a fixed rule, so that two implementations agree. **The kind** is the first, in this precedence, among the contributors' kinds: `motivated_by` (3), then `contingent` (0), `triggered` (1), `enabled` (4), `probabilistic` (5), `relayed_knowledge` (2): richness first, so a motivated edge keeps its motive, weight and margin; then an explicit reference, a mechanical consequence, an enabling condition, a hazard share, a testimony hop. The three input kinds have no writer and never coalesce (`append` refuses them). **The weight** is the saturating `Q8_8` sum of the contributors' weights when the kept kind is `motivated_by`, else 0. **The motive** is the one with the largest |weight| among the motivated contributors (a tie goes to the lower `[motive_edges]` index; an unnamed contributor counts as the highest index) when the kept kind is `motivated_by`, else NULL. **The evidence** is the largest among the contributors of the kept kind: a kept `motivated_by` keeps its margin rather than a merged mechanical link's 1, and a kept deterministic kind has 1 by rule. After coalescing, edges are ordered by `cause_id` ascending (canonical form), and `append` refuses a repeated cause.

**`cause_told`** (`05.2` §4.0; **D38**). Every draft gets an id; under the admission rule only admitted events get a row. An edge whose cause was refused keeps the refused id, and the row says so: `cause_told BOOLEAN NOT NULL`. It is **not** part of the event (not hashed: §6); the Chronicle Writer computes it when it writes the `EVENT` record, from an admission bitset it keeps per timeline (host memory, one bit per `EventId`, rebuilt from the sealed `event` tables at resume), so it needs no plane-A memory and no read-back. In every P1a world every draft is admitted (`05.2` §4.0: every P1a world is under the ceiling; P1.0 admits everything by rule), so `cause_told` is `true` on every P1a row whichever way D38 goes; only the kingdom worlds (P1.11) can write `false`.

## 6. What enters `state_hash`

`rh-core/events.meta.running` folds `Event::digest()` of every event in id order (`05.1` §6 item 6; SPR-006 G4). SPR-020 changes the digest to version 2, tag `rehoboam/rh-events/log-hash/v2`, over these words (little-endian `u64`s through `h64_words`, as v1):

```text
id, tick, phase, h(emitter), h(kind),
has_place (0|1), place (0 when absent), magnitude (i32 sign-extended), secrecy, origin,
n_roles,     then per role row:  role, entity_kind_tag, entity_raw
n_snapshots, then per snapshot:  h(attr name), entity_kind_tag, entity_raw, has_partner (0|1), partner_kind_tag, partner_raw (0, 0 when absent), value (i32 sign-extended)
n_causes,    then per edge:      cause_id, kind, has_motive (0|1), h(motive name) (0 when absent), weight (i16 sign-extended), evidence
n_payload,   then the payload words
```

So **every stored field of an event enters `state_hash`**: the event-level `place_id`, `magnitude`, `secrecy` and `origin` (R3: places landing in SPR-018 need no v3), roles with their codes, every snapshot by its attribute's name (R4), every edge's kind, motive name, weight and evidence. **Two things do not:** the admission flag and `cause_told`, because `05.2` §4.0 fixes that the counter and the running hash do not depend on admission (plane A never branches on the flag; the chronicle is a filter on the stream of ids), and because `cause_told` is a function of admission alone. A timer's, a command's and a queued input's `origin` (§2) are hashed where they live (`agenda.timers`, `engine.commands`, `engine.inputs`) as a tail present only when non-zero (R13, R23, R24): hash-neutral at `sim`, so every row that exists today hashes as before, and a row with an input's origin moves exactly when it should.

**Effect on the baselines (the C3 part of SPR-020).** The digest of an event with no snapshot, no motive and the P1.0 edge shape is still not the v1 digest: a role row now carries its code, an edge its kind and two zeros, and the event its four level fields. The alternative, a v1-compatible encoding that adds no bytes for `actor` rows and `triggered` edges, was rejected (3/10): it would make the hash blind to exactly the mislabelling the envelope is meant to make visible (a deceased hashed as `actor` would hash as before), and the precedent for a hash-neutral slot (EVO-001 R15) was an *empty* slot, not a default value. Therefore:

- **`smoke` changes**, in these tables and no other. (i) `rh-core/events.meta`, from its first event (`CENSUS` at the first Month pulse): the v2 digest. (ii) `rh-core/engine.inputs`: smoke journals and submits all of its synthetic inputs (`OracleMove` rows, so `oracle`) before the first instant, and a queued input carries its origin (§2), so the table differs from the first rung until the last input is applied (year 994). (iii) `rh-core/agenda.timers` and `rh-core/engine.commands` only at instants inside an input year: the re-sampled X3 timers land within about sixty days of the input on day 100 and their `Settle` and the X4 `Audit` drain within days, so no `oracle` row is pending at a year end. **Prediction, to be measured** (R23; nothing implements the origin yet): at monthly granularity the bisect (V-DET-11, `rh-golden`, tables in name order) names `rh-core/engine.inputs` at month 1, before `rh-core/events.meta`; the yearly ladder differs at every rung from 1 to 994 in `engine.inputs` and from rung 1 in `events.meta`, and in nothing else; `agenda.timers` and `engine.commands` differ only on a monthly ladder over an input year (year 7, months 4 to 6), or in an engine test, never at a yearly rung. **Acceptance** (the robust form): SPR-020's audit records the measured first divergence and the set of differing tables at every rung, and every differing table is one this section names (`events.meta`, `engine.inputs`, `agenda.timers`, `engine.commands`); a world column, a draw or any other engine table in the set is a defect. SPR-020 regenerates `worlds/smoke/baselines/smoke_yearly_1000y.ladder` in its final `[C3]` commit with `bin/regen_baseline.sh`.
- **`solo`, `household` and `hamlet`** (SPR-013's and SPR-012's baselines, recorded before SPR-020 lands) change in their **state ladders** as the same prediction says: `events.meta` from their first event (in `solo`, its one `DEATH`: `DEATH` rows gain the `target` code, `EXTINCTION`'s edge gains `kind = triggered`, every event its four level fields); and `engine.inputs` wherever an input is queued: `solo` holds one scope-only `OracleMove` on Ada from before the first instant (`worlds/solo/inputs.toml`, SPR-012), so its `engine.inputs` differs from rung 1 until the input is applied, and the bisect names it before `events.meta`; `household` and `hamlet` queue what their input files hold. Their **channel-A segments keep their shape** (R6): SPR-013 writes channel A in the amended v0 shape (this PR), which is final. Their canonical digests still move, in exactly these lines and no others (Architect text ruling, 2026-10-17, at SPR-020's plan): `STEP_DIGEST`'s and `KEYFRAME`'s `state_hash`, which follow the state ladder; `KEYFRAME`'s `keyframe_format`, which becomes `v2` (§7); and `EVENT`'s `origin`, for an event whose causal chain starts at a journaled input (§2). Every other line is byte-identical to SPR-013's writer.

The keyframe format becomes `rehoboam/rh-snapshot/keyframe/v2` (R12 (d)): a v1 keyframe that holds undrained events cannot be decoded by a v2 reader, and one that holds none is refused by the format name, which says which decoder it needs; envelope, guard and layers are unchanged.

## 7. Provenance of change (SAGA-15) and the plane-B index

**The typed value rule** (R2, R17). A column that records damage or transformation (`Ruin { cause }`, `Scar { cause, site }`, a captured or cursed artifact, a burned field) stores its cause inside the value: in the column model of `[[owns]]`, as a sibling column `<column>_cause` of type **`event_ref`** owned by the same system, declared on the state column as `cause_column = "<entity>.<column>_cause"` together with the state column's **declared default** (`default = ...`, typed by the column: `1.0` for `artifact.condition`, `0` for a contaminant bitset, a value name for an enum, since an enum's code 0 is meaningful). An `event_ref` column is an `Option<EventId>`, none by default: `rh-events` assigns ids from 0, so "0 = none" would collide with the first event (an artifact placed at world creation can be caused by event 0); the presence bit is the option's: `opt(u64)` is the encoding `rh-snapshot` defines for its engine tables, and a world column is today eight bytes per row (`i64`), so the `event_ref` world-column row encoding (a presence bit beside the id, or `opt(u64)` per row) is the sprint's to fix in `rh-snapshot`'s layout, with the hash golden regenerated; a segment stores it as a nullable column; no event is renumbered (R17). An `event_ref` column declares no `default` (its default is none by definition; `lint_odd` refuses one: R26). Several state columns may share one cause column when one act changes them together (S6's `condition_cause` serves `condition`, `contaminants` and `charge`). **The slot writes any `event_ref` column** (R25): a column whose value *is* an event the same step emits (S6's `chain_head`, the latest chain event, and `origin_event` at `ARTIFACT_FORGED`; M7's `claim.cause_event`, the `SUCCEED` or `DEPOSED` that created the claim) is typed `event_ref` and written through `ctx.set_cause(column, row, EventRef)` with the draft's `DraftRef`, never as an integer in the step; the three are retyped in this PR (M7 within its 0.3.3 bump, S6 a draft). A column that only ever receives an id the step *read* (T1's `opinion.source_event` and `thought.source_event`, filled from a command's `EventRef` payload the merge already resolved) may stay a `u64`, as a `Known` id is an integer. An `EventId` exists only after the merge and a `DraftRef` is not an integer (`05.1` §2 rule 2), so a step never writes the cause column itself (`SystemView::write` hands no `event_ref` column to a step): it calls **`ctx.set_cause(column, row, EventRef)`**, a buffered slot like a timer's `cause` (EVO-001 R15), with `Draft(r)` for the act it emitted in this step or `Known(id)` for an id it read; the merge rewrites the slot to the id and writes the cause column, events before causes, so the id is always smaller than any later event's. **The post-merge check** (R22 NIT-4): before a level runs, the engine keeps the rows of every column with a `cause_column` that the level's jobs write, as `LifeCourseGuard` keeps the life-course rows; after the merge, every kept row whose value changed to something other than its declared default, and **every row the level inserted** whose value is not the default (a new artifact forged with `condition` below 1: an inserted row counts as changed from the default, R26), has a present cause, else `SimError::ChangeWithoutCause`, naming the system, the column and the row (V-SAGA-21's engine side). A system with such a column names the event kind that causes each change in the column's `meaning`. `S6.artifacts` declares it in this PR (`condition_cause` is an `event_ref`; `charge`, `condition` and `contaminants` carry their `default` and `cause_column`; its `condition_cause` text is corrected: the cause is set through `set_cause` with the act's `DraftRef`, never typed as an id in the step). `lint_odd.py` accepts `cause_column` and `default` on `[[owns]]` from this PR and checks that the cause column is an owned `event_ref` of the same entity and that the default is typed by the column; `rh-registry`'s `COLUMN_TYPES` gains `event_ref` so that the Rust spec loader accepts S6 (the vocabulary only; the column's storage is the sprint's). `05.1` §2 rule 4 names the slot and the guard (v1.13, this PR). **There is no `last_mutating_event_id` column on tiles, settlements or persons**: a place's history is read from the chronicle (next).

**The "what last happened here?" index (plane B).** DuckDB macros in `schema.sql` over the Parquet chronicle, never a plane-A column:

| Macro | Answers |
|---|---|
| `last_event_at_place(tl, place_id, t)` | the latest admitted event at `place_id` with `tick <= t` (`event.place_id`) |
| `last_event_in_cell(tl, attr_x, attr_y, x, y, t)` | the same for a cell, through the `cell_x` and `cell_y` snapshots (their `attr_id`s are the Registry indexes, passed in) |
| `last_event_for(tl, entity_kind, entity_id, t)` | the latest event in whose cast the entity appears (`event_role`), with its role |
| `events_at_place(tl, place_id, t0, t1)` | the window form of the first, for the inspector |

`selftest_envelope.py` exercises them on a synthetic chronicle (§9); the reviewer checked them against brute force on 3,000 queries with same-tick ties. A viewer, the inspector and the saga pipeline read them; plane A never does (V-PLN-01).

## 8. The Registry (BCK-002 A-24, the envelope part)

`rh-manifest`'s `Registry` (the typed loader; `rh-registry` stays plane A and reads the compiled table) types three more sections, unknown fields refused as for every typed section, and `Registry::problems` gains these refusals, in its stable order:

| Section | Type | `problems` refuses |
|---|---|---|
| `[event_roles]` | `IdList` (`ids: Vec<String>`) | an empty list; a duplicate (the generic rule); an id not `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*`; a list whose first four ids are not `actor, target, witness, instrument` in that order (the codes `schema.sql`, channel A and the `life_ends` twin read as literals 0 and 1; a role is only ever appended, and reordering is a C3 change) |
| `[motive_edges]` | `IdList` | an empty list; a duplicate; an id not `[A-Z][A-Z0-9_]*`; an id that is also a `[verbs]` key (a `tail` line and a pattern name a motive and a verb in the same style, and must never confuse them) |
| `[role_domains]` (new, R1, R16) | `BTreeMap<String, RoleDomain { attribute: String, relational: bool, owner: String, column: String }>` | a key that is not a `[roles]` id, or a `[roles]` id other than `any` without an entry; a key other than the four; an `attribute` that is not a `bool` in `[attributes]`, or whose arity does not match `relational` (one argument, or `actor, partner`); an `owner` that is neither a `[systems]` id, `"life_course"` nor `"none"`; a `column` that is not `entity.column`, `algorithm.<name>` or `life_course`, or that is not `"none"` exactly when the owner is `none`. `owner` is the system that owns the source column and `column` what the attribute is read from; any emitter that reads it may declare the snapshot (R16 split the earlier `source` into the two) |
| `[attributes]` | already typed | nothing changes in the table; it gains the domain attributes (`holds_title`, `is_successor`, `is_ally`, `is_rival`, `is_subordinate`, `is_member`, `is_investigator`, `is_suspect`, `is_host`, `is_guest`; `is_self_or_kin` exists). The attribute index is its position in the table sorted by name, computed by the loader (`Registry::attr_id(name)`) and written to the segment dictionary; the digest hashes the name, never the index |

`lint_odd.py` and `lint_patterns.py` read the same sections from the TOML; `lint_patterns.py` refuses a Registry without `[event_roles]` or `[role_domains]` by name (R12 NIT-4) and one whose `[role_domains]` has another shape (R22 NIT-3; its self-check plants a non-relational role on a two-argument attribute with an unknown owner). The smoke overlay may add none of these sections (an overlay is `[systems]` and `[verbs]` only; the smoke world uses the Registry's roles).

## 9. The byte budget, and the test

`05.2` §4.1a budgets about 35 bytes per event beyond what the event already holds (about 43 with cells and contaminants); V-SAGA-15 gates a **mean stored** overhead of at most 40 (48) bytes per event, measured on the 10k dynastic world over 100 years (D34). Two measures, both stated here so that the gate is unambiguous:

- **Engine bytes** (the `events.held` encoding of §6, which is also what the running hash covers): the four level fields cost 11 (`opt(u32)` place, `i32`, `u8`, `u8`); a role row beyond `actor` and `target` 10 (`u8 role`, `u8 kind`, `u64 id`); a snapshot 4 + the attribute name's length + 9 (+ 10 with a partner) + 4; a typed edge 6 more than P1.0's bare `u64` (14 in all; 16 plus the motive name's length with a motive). P1a's fullest event (a T1 act: one `stress` snapshot, one motive edge, no witness) adds about 60 bytes over P1.0 in engine memory. The §4.1a figure for emotion (16 bytes for two actors) assumed a packed PAD‖OCC‖intensity value; as five Registry attributes they would cost about 100 raw bytes per actor at P4, which is why the stored measure is the gate: if it breaks the budget then, a packed `emotion` attribute type (one row) is the fix, a C2 change at P4, not now.
- **Stored bytes** (what V-SAGA-15 measures; **fixed here for SPR-030**, R11): the Parquet bytes, zstd at the writer's level, of **(i)** `event_role` rows beyond the first two per event, **(ii)** every `event_snapshot` row, **(iii)** every `causal_edge` row whose kind is `motivated_by`, in full (a motive edge is new: §4.1a counts the kind and the cause id), and **(iv)** the `motive`, `weight`, `evidence` and `cause_told` columns of every other edge (P1.0 already stored their `cause_id` and `kind`), divided by the number of `EVENT` records. The reference measure is check 6 of `selftest_envelope.py`, which writes a synthetic 100-year chronicle at the P1a design densities (a quarter of the events are T1 acts with a `stress` snapshot and a motive edge, half of the deaths and successions carry a `triggered` edge, a tenth of the events have a witness, a fifth carry cell coordinates; the densities are constants of the file) through DuckDB's Parquet writer and asserts the mean at or under 40: it measures **4.0 bytes per event**, and **14.3** when every event carries the fullest P1a envelope (a witness, a `stress` snapshot and a motive edge), against the 48-byte ceiling; a planted control (`--control budget-bloat`, fifty witnesses on every event) must fail it. Dictionary and delta encoding make the stored cost a fraction of the engine bytes.

## 11. Alternatives considered

- *Channel A v1 instead of amending v0.* No build writes channel A yet (SPR-013 has not started its code), no segment exists, and the golden canonical file is tool-generated; a v1 would make `rh-stream` carry two formats from its first day. **Amend v0 in place (R6):** both files' status rules now say that a change before the first build writes a segment is an in-place amendment recorded in the file's change log, and a new version after that; `05.2` §4.0's `cause_told` sentence and SPR-013's sprint document (the amendment drafted in this PR: the three child tables and six dictionaries, 16 files per segment, most of them empty in P1a) say the same.
- *Abstract roles derived at the event's tick from intervals* (round 1). Rejected by the review (MAJ-1): unanchored within an instant. Replaced by domain attributes captured as snapshots (R1).
- *A step storing its own event's id in a cause column* (round 1). Rejected (MAJ-2): the id does not exist in the step. Replaced by the buffered `set_cause` slot (R2).
- *Engine-filled snapshots* (the merge reads the Registry's attribute sources after the level). Rejected: the value at the merge is not what the rule read (the emitter's lagged, pre-act view is), which breaks DATA-3's letter and V-DATA-08's oracle; and it puts a Registry-driven column read into `rh-core`. 2/10.
- *Edge kind codes per motive* (9..17). Rejected: a Registry list would move codes, and the canonical form fixes 0–8. 1/10.
- *`cause_told` as plane-A state.* Rejected: a per-`EventId` admission map in the engine is unbounded memory in plane A for a fact the writer already knows. 2/10.
- *A v1-compatible event digest* (§6). Rejected, 3/10, for blindness to mislabelled roles.
- *Coalescing with `contingent` first* (the ruling's example order, R21). Not taken, 6/10: a `REVENGE` that names the harm it answers carries both a `contingent` reference and a `RETALIATION_FOR` motive to that harm, and `contingent` first would drop the motive, the weight and the margin from exactly the richest edge; `motivated_by` first keeps them, and the reference stays legible from the verb's `references`.
- *`EventId + 1` in a `u64` cause column* (R17). Rejected, 4/10: it keeps "0 = none" at the price of an off-by-one every reader must know; an `event_ref` is the option itself and uses the keyframe's existing `opt(u64)`.
