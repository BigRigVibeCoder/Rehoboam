# 05.3 — Cognition, Epistemics, Persuasion and Foresight

*Status: Draft v1.1 (2026-10-15; revised after the red-team review). Sources: R04, R05, R09, red-team RT-M1, M2, M5, M13, m3. Decisions: ADR-0012–0015, 0024, 0025, 0034.*
*v1.2 (2026-10-16): deliberation consults retired; named actors decide only through the simulation (ADR-0034, D27).*
*v1.3 (2026-10-16), ADR-0035: the minds system's Registry id is `T1.minds`. Secrets v0 carries the information horizon (SAGA-4): knowledge spreads only through scheduled outcomes, and no actor reacts before it knows. **Minds v0 moves to P1a** (traits, ambition, relationships, opinions, IAUS for a few decision types) with **stress and mental breaks** (§2.1); secrets v0 (knowledge rows for flagged hidden acts) lands in P1b; the full core, HTN, beliefs, channels and the Oracle follow in P4. Artifacts (`S6.artifacts`) and vows (`S7.vows`) reach a mind only through lifecycle commands (`ApplyInfluence`, `AgendaGoal`, `OpinionModifier`, `ApplyThought`); a vow's weight never enters a survival decision.*

## 1. Minds by tier

| Tier | Population | State | Decision mechanism | Cadence | LLM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Masses (pops)** | 100k–10M persons via ~10³–10⁴ pops (region × class × faith) | Distributions of morale, fear, needs satisfaction, faction loyalty; **belief shares** per tracked proposition; omen credibility | Difference equations: needs → unrest; compartment rumour flow (ignorant → spreader → stifler); bounded-confidence drift; Granovetter thresholds (M6) | Month (+ event interrupts) | None at runtime |
| **T1 Named actors** | 1k–5k: rulers, active great-house members, officers, clergy, office-holding or investigated hive nodes, dragons. Promotion only by **simulation criteria or a journaled input**, never by pinning (ADR-0024) | Psychological core (§2) + overlays; beliefs; memories; relationships | **Event-driven IAUS** chooses goals and stratagems; **bounded HTN** decomposes them; places and institutions advertise (05.1 §4.3 semantics) | CK3-style polling, 1–12 months per decision type, plus interrupts at ≥ t + 1 day with a daily cap | None in decisions (ADR-0034) |
| **T3 Embodied** | ≤ 200 in the bubble (Live speed only) | Plane-A row + T1 mind if named; otherwise a **light mind**, a pure function of plane A (§1.1) | **A view (plane B):** schedules plus scheduled outcomes plus T1 intents. A behaviour tree animates it | 10–20 Hz (presentation) | No |
| **T4 Conversant** | 1–3 | As T3 + dialogue buffer | **The sim plans the speech act; the LLM realises it** (§8) | Turn-based | Local model |

### 1.1 Light minds for unnamed persons
When an unnamed ledger person is hydrated or spoken to, they get a light mind:

- **Traits** from `H(seed, id, "trait", k)`, mapped through the pop's trait distribution.
- **Beliefs** seeded from the pop's belief shares. Whether this person holds a proposition is a keyed Bernoulli draw, so it is stable across visits.
- **Memories** synthesised as references to *real* region and household events since birth: famines, raids, deaths of kin.

Light minds are **pure functions of plane A** (`H`, pop shares and the life-course log), so recomputing them is free and always consistent. Whatever a conversation *showed you* goes to the plane-B facts ledger, which is the pin. Being observed never promotes a light mind to T1. Promotion happens only through simulation criteria or a **journaled material input**: for example, you hire the person, accuse them, or they witness a material event you caused.

## 2. The generic psychological core

The core is setting-agnostic. Values are fixed-point.

| Component | Representation | Dynamics | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traits | 6 × HEXACO (i8, −100..100) | Static: no drift in v0 (P1a). Artifacts and curses never write them; they act through thoughts (below) | Ashton & Lee |
| Ambition | One v0 scalar (0..100), static (`T1.minds` spec) | The drive for seats, offices and claims; an IAUS input | Design (minds v0, P3.2a) |
| Values | 10 × Schwartz basic values (i8) | Slow drift from culture and experience | Schwartz |
| Mood | PAD (3 × i16) | Pulled by emotions; decays to a trait-derived baseline (ALMA) | Gebhard |
| Emotions | ≤ 8 OCC slots {type, intensity u8, half-life} | Appraisal of events against goals and standards; exponential decay | OCC, GAMYGDALA |
| Needs | 5–7 meters (sustenance, safety, belonging, esteem, autonomy, purpose…) | Decay + satisfaction from actions and advertisements | Sims, DF |
| Opinions | Per relationship edge: list of `OpinionModifier {source_event, magnitude, decay}`; opinion = Σ | Each modifier decays or expires | CK3 |
| Thoughts | `Thought {vector deltas, half-life, stack_limit, stack_mult, source}` | Timed modifiers. This is where whispers and omens land. In v0 they are the **bounded channel** for artifacts and curses: an `ApplyInfluence` or `ApplyThought` command becomes a thought on an overlay, capped at `thought_cap` and decaying by `thought_decay`, and IAUS reads trait + thought while the trait columns stay static (RT-2026-10-16 item 8; `T1.minds` spec) | RimWorld |
| Beliefs | Sparse store (§4) | Updated by channels | Talk of the Town (Ryan et al. 2015) |
| Memories | References to `event_id` + encoding intensity | ACT-R base-level activation `B = ln Σ t_j^{−d}` | Anderson & Schooler |

**Setting overlays** are declared in the manifest and Registry as *derived read-outs* that the UI and the sifter can use:

| Overlay | Derivation (initial definitions; tune in calibration) |
|---|---|
| Despair | Mood region P < θ for > τ, plus unmet needs |
| Paranoia | Trait (low Agreeableness, high Emotionality) + state (fear + suspicion beliefs). **It also sets the accusation criterion** |
| Hubris | Low Honesty-Humility + high Dominance + success streak |
| Filial Piety | Tradition + Benevolence values × kin-edge weight |
| Corruption | Accumulated corrupt acts gated by low Honesty-Humility (setting stat) |

The five draft vectors survive as **UI readouts**. The simulation runs on the core.

### 2.1 Stress and mental breaks (ADR-0035; minds v0, P1a)
The design is inspired by Crusader Kings 3; no code is taken from it.
- **Stress** is a `q8_8` column per named actor, on a 0–100 scale, owned by `T1.minds`. It rises when a vow, kin loyalty or a value conflicts with the situation: being made to serve a father's murderer, sharing a hall with the target of a vow. `S7.vows` publishes the vow-conflict signal (`vow.conflict`), which minds reads as a rate through a bounded, sourced in-coupling (to be written in the `T1.minds` spec). Stress decays otherwise.
- **A mental break** is a scheduled outcome (a timer, ADR-0025). Crossing the Registry threshold is a deterministic state transition that schedules it, at a delay drawn with keyed RNG. When it fires, the actor drops rational self-preservation for one volatile act from the Registry break table (`[mental_breaks]`: an assassination attempt, flight, burning their holdings, a pact of despair), and stress drops by that act's relief. Flight makes the actor a fugitive (SAGA-14).
- **Deterministic and journaled.** The act's consequences are simulated like any other action; the simulation decides (ADR-0034). V-SAGA-16 checks the accounting and the break frequency.

## 3. Decision architecture

1. **Decision types.** Each type has an IAUS consideration set and a polling interval. Examples: `choose_stratagem`, `respond_to_threat`, `marry`, `appoint`, `rebel`, `purge`, `confess`, `investigate`.
2. **IAUS scoring.** Score = ∏ response_curve_i(input_i) × compensation factor, over candidate (action, target) pairs. Response curves are **fixed-point lookup tables**, and same-instant semantics follow 05.1 §4.3. Inputs come from mind state, beliefs (*not* world truth), relationships and anchored world state.
   - Selection is **top-k weighted** with keyed RNG. The pure argmax looks robotic.
   - The **top contributors and the margin over the runner-up** are logged as `motivated_by` edges.
3. **HTN.** Small method libraries expand a stratagem into a plan, e.g. `assassinate(rival)` → recruit agent → acquire poison → gain access → act. Plans are re-planned **only on interrupts**.
4. **Advertisements.** Places and institutions (temple, tavern, court, market) advertise need satisfaction, Sims-style, for low-level activity choice.
5. **Contingent unlocking.** Registry actions declare which past-event roles they can reference: a `revenge` action unlocks for victims of a `harm` event. These references become *contingency* causal edges. This is the narratability lever (R02 §R2).

**Cost.** 5,000 actors × 30 options × 5 considerations ≈ 750k curve evaluations, ≈ 1.9 ms in Rust with rayon. That is R03's measured 0.81 ms for a 320k-evaluation toy (5k actors × 16 actions × 4 considerations) scaled by 750k / 320k; R04 estimates ≈ 7.5 ms on one core. Event-driven decisions at monthly cadence over 1,000 years total about 6×10⁷ decisions, ≈ 60–300 CPU-s.

## 4. Epistemics

### 4.1 Belief record
`Belief {subject, predicate, object|value, conf: i8 log-odds, evidence: {observation, reflection, statement, eavesdrop, transference, confabulation, lie, mutation, vision}, source_agent, channel, t_acquired, t_last_reinforced, event_ref, lineage_ptr}`

### 4.2 Update rule
`conf += trust(source) × fidelity(channel) × relevance × skepticism(Paranoia, in-group)`, all clamped.

- A contradicted belief triggers dissonance appraisal (anger or shame).
- Low-activation memories become eligible for **mutation** or **transference** (seeded), which produces misremembering.

### 4.3 Channels (the hive is one of them)

| Channel | Latency | Fidelity per hop | Trust weight | Reach | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Witness (co-presence) | 0 | High; drops with distance and stress | Self ≈ 1 | Place × time | Witness sets come from co-presence. Raycasts only in the bubble |
| Testimony / gossip | Hours–days | 0.6–0.9 + distortion | Relationship trust | Social-graph neighbours | Driven by "tell" actions |
| Document / decree | Days–weeks | ≈ 1 content, low interpretation | Institutional legitimacy | Literate subset | Forgery = a lie on an artifact |
| Crowd (sermon, market) | Days | Low | Faction affinity | Pops | Compartment flows into pop belief shares |
| Oracle whisper | 0 | 1 (gated by the parse) | Trust in the "inner voice" | One target | §6 |
| **Hive** | **0** | **1** | **1** | **All drones and mimics** | One shared store; members have no copies of their own |

### 4.4 Distortion operators
These are deterministic transforms on structured propositions, each applied with a seeded probability:

- **Leveling:** drop an attribute.
- **Sharpening:** scale a magnitude.
- **Assimilation:** substitute an actor consistent with the listener's stereotypes and out-group.

Each transform yields a new `variant_id` linked to the original through `lineage_ptr`. That lineage is what lets the chronicle show *how* a rumour mutated.

### 4.5 Theory of mind
- Depth-1 beliefs ("X believes P") are stored only for **flagged propositions**: plots, affairs, mimic suspicion, secrets.
- Depth-2 ("X believes Y suspects me") is stored only for active schemes.
- **Suspicion** is a Bayesian posterior over each other actor's hidden type (human or mimic, loyal or plotter), updated with the likelihood of their observed actions under each type's policy.

## 5. Hive and mimics

- **Hive objective:** `U = Σ w_b·biomass + Σ w_i·g(infiltrated) − λ·Σ drones_lost − μ·E[exposure_cost]`, discounted at γ, with concave g.
- **Mimic utility** = cover maintenance (act as their pre-capture self) + covert objectives. Both are gated by local risk, measured as witness density and suspicion of them.
- **Tells**, each with its own sensitivity and specificity:
  - **Impossible-knowledge events**: an action draws on hive-only knowledge.
  - **Behavioural drift**: the KL divergence of recent action choices from the pre-capture baseline.
  - **Physical tests**, which are imperfect and carry se/sp.
- **Detection dynamics** (M5 coupling):
  - Positives = τ_test·(se·I_c + (1−sp)·S).
  - At low prevalence, PPV collapses: 0.1% prevalence with a 99%/99% test makes 91% of positives false.
  - Paranoia lowers investigators' accusation criterion.
  - Purges of false positives cut legitimacy and raise fear.
  - Hive test-evasion lowers the effective se.

  None of this is scripted. The witch-hunt spiral emerges from these rules.
- **Scale.** Hive members are a ledger flag plus **squad aggregates**. Only office-holding nodes and nodes flagged by investigations are individual T1 actors. At 10% prevalence in a 1M city, 100k individual nodes would be infeasible.

## 6. Whisper Counsel: parse, don't inject

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  T[Player text] --> PF[Pre-filter<br/>≤500 chars, strip markup]
  PF --> P1[Parser LLM, temp 0, JSON grammar<br/>text quoted as DATA, no tools]
  PF --> P2[Meta-instruction classifier<br/>independent model/pass]
  P1 --> AF[ArgumentFrame]
  P2 --> AF
  AF --> G[Ground<br/>claims vs ORACLE knowledge: verified / lie / bluff<br/>claims vs TARGET beliefs: consistent / contradicting / novel]
  G --> S[Score - deterministic, no LLM]
  S --> AP[Apply Thought modifier<br/>+ beliefs with source=inner voice]
  AP --> SH[Show parse to player]
  AF --> J[(Input journal)]
```

**ArgumentFrame.** `{appeals: [type ∈ {evidence, value(v), fear, hope, authority, kinship, threat, promise, social_proof}], target_vectors: [...], requested_action?: registry_action_id, claims: [(s, p, o)], references: [entity/event names], meta_instruction: bool}`. When the two passes disagree, the weaker reading wins.

**Scoring** (no LLM):

```
p_central  = σ(a·Openness + b·Involvement − c·Despair − d·Stress)
central    = evidence_strength × VAF_congruence(target value ranking, invoked value)
peripheral = trust(inner_voice) × emotional_fit(current OCC state)
raw        = p_central·central + (1−p_central)·peripheral
Δ          = raw × 1/(1 + k·n_recent_same_vector)               # habituation
           × min(1, c_max·(1 − e^(−Foresight_spent/F₀)))         # cap
           × r,  r ~ Beta(α(Hubris, Agreeableness), β(Paranoia)) # seeded resistance
if requested_action violates target's top values or autonomy → Δ := −Δ (reactance)
if meta_instruction → Δ := 0; Paranoia += m; memory("uncanny voice")
```

**Apply.** A `Thought` modifier with half-life, stack limit and stacking multiplier. Verified claims become beliefs with `channel = whisper`. A **caught lie** is a claim that contradicts a high-confidence target belief, and it erodes trust in the voice.

**Show.** *"Heard as an appeal to filial duty, citing the famine of 409."* The display includes the target's reaction only if **the Oracle knows it** (§6.2), e.g. *"She already doubted the fire"* appears only if you learned that earlier. Showing the parse turns guessing into play and makes injection visibly pointless.

**Worst case (SEC-1).** A perfect argument is already capped, so the worst injection gets at most the cap, and it is penalised in-world.

### 6.1 Pierce the Veil and Plant Omen
- **Pierce the Veil** adds a belief to the **Oracle's** store with `evidence_type = vision`. It reveals hidden state (hive membership, secret loyalties). NPCs cannot verify a vision: to act on it, you have to convince someone in-world with evidence the target can check.
- **Plant Omen** uses a pop-level Bayesian-persuasion receiver with a per-pop **omen credibility**. Omens shift morale and fear through compartment flows. Credibility decays with frequency and with portents that fail. Priests (T1) *interpret* omens according to their faction's utility, so the same omen can be spun two ways.
- **Forbidden Ward** (fantasy registries only) is a physical-field effect at a coordinate, with duration in sim-time.

### 6.2 The Oracle's knowledge, and the two console modes (RT-M13)
- **The Oracle is a plane-A entity with its own belief store.** It is fed through the same channels as everyone else: witnessing (co-location), testimony from informants, documents, and `vision` from Pierce.
- **Evidence you can cite** in a whisper is limited to what the Oracle's store holds. Grounding (§6) checks claims against it.
- The console has two modes:

| Mode | What you see | Oracle verbs |
|---|---|---|
| **Cultivator** | Omniscient: the full causal inspector, secrets, hive membership | Disabled. If enabled, they are flagged "omniscient" in the journal and excluded from Oracle-mode play. |
| **Oracle** | Every view (map, inspector, chronicle, Foresight) is **filtered by the Oracle's knowledge**. Secret events and hidden attributes appear only if the Oracle knows them. | Enabled |

### 6.3 Persuasion balance (RT-m3)
- **Oracle leverage** is the ΔP (Foresight, common random numbers) per whisper, per unit of Foresight spent. It has target bands, e.g. 5–15 points for a strong, verified whisper on a receptive target and under 3 for a weak or bluffed one.
- **Parameter sweeps** over the scoring constants are tuned against those bands.
- An **adversarial player bot** maximises ΔP per Foresight across red-team texts and argument strategies. It runs as a CI gate: no strategy may exceed the cap envelope.

## 7. Foresight: Monte-Carlo prophecy

1. **The prophecy.** You write a predicate over the future, e.g. `crowned(Valen) before 1250 AND NOT civil_war(realm, 1240..1250)`. A prophecy DSL shares its grammar with the sifter.
2. **Fork.** The Foresight Orchestrator forks the canonical timeline at the current tick. Each rollout extends the salt lineage uniquely (05.1 §6.9). Pre-sampled timers whose purpose is flagged `resample_on_fork` are re-drawn, so rollouts are not under-dispersed.
   - **In Oracle mode, rollouts fork from a belief-resampled state.** Hidden attributes the Oracle doesn't know (secret plots, hive membership) are re-drawn per rollout from the Oracle's posterior, keyed by salt. Foresight therefore never leaks hidden truth. **Cultivator** mode forks from the true state.
3. **Run.** N rollouts (default 256) run headless at MAX on rollout workers. They scale horizontally across cores and nodes.
   - The run is truncated at the predicate's horizon.
   - Cost is reduced where the predicate cannot be affected, through **cadence and richness** (coarser timers, fewer named-actor decision types), never by switching representation (05.1 §3).
   - At 1M persons Foresight is a **batch job** (minutes on a cluster node; `08` §3). Interactive Foresight targets 100k-class worlds.
4. **Estimate.** `P = hits/N` with a Wilson interval. **Failure-path clustering** groups failing rollouts by their sifted-arc signatures ("regicide 41%, campaign death 22%, partition war 19%").
5. **Intervention evaluation.** The candidate intervention runs on the **same salts** as the baseline (common random numbers). The paired difference ΔP has far lower variance than two independent ensembles.
6. **Validity tests** (RT-M5; per-decile reliability with about 20 prophecies per bucket cannot pass even for a perfect forecaster). These run nightly on the cluster:
   - **(a) Exchangeability.** A rank histogram or PIT of the canonical continuation among the rollouts. It must be uniform (χ² test).
   - **(b) Reduced-cost bias.** CRN-paired comparison of reduced-cost against full-cost rollouts: |ΔP| ≤ 0.03.
   - **(c) Scoring.** Brier and log score with bootstrap CIs, plus a Spiegelhalter calibration test. In Oracle mode these also measure the information loss from belief resampling.
7. **The game mechanic.** Running Foresight costs the Oracle **Foresight**, which regenerates per sim-time. The cost scales with N and horizon. Larger ensembles on more compute buy tighter intervals, which is a direct use of unbounded compute.
8. **The canonical timeline is never changed by Foresight.** Only journaled interventions change it.

## 8. LLM touchpoints in cognition

| Touchpoint | Input | Output (schema) | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dialogue planning** | *Simulation*: speaker beliefs (activation-ranked), secrets, relationship to the listener, current emotion, utility of lying | `SpeechPlan {act: reveal/conceal/lie/deflect/gossip/ask/threaten/promise, belief_ids[], conceal_ids[], stance, emotion}`, decided by the simulation (IAUS over speech acts) | Authoritative (sim) |
| **Dialogue realisation** | SpeechPlan + persona card (voice sheet) + belief texts for `belief_ids` only + last turns | Prose (streamed) | Cosmetic |
| **Claim check** | Prose → extracted claims | Each claim maps to `belief_ids ∪ planned_lies` or is rejected and regenerated (≤ 2), then falls back to a template | Gate |
| **Speech-act extraction (player utterance)** | Player text | `SpeechAct {type ∈ {ASK, INFORM, ACCUSE, THREATEN, PROMISE, CONFESS}, subject, predicate, object, evidence_refs}` | **Material input**, journaled with a dependency scope (usually the listener) and applied in the `Inputs` phase (05.2 §3.2, §5) |
| **Whisper parser** | §6 | ArgumentFrame | Journaled input |

**Determinism.** Every translated LLM output that changes state (a speech act, a whisper frame) is journaled as a push input with model ID, weights hash, prompt hash and output. On replay it is read from the journal and never regenerated. Batch-invariant inference is *not* required, because outputs are recorded rather than reproduced.

**Named actors decide through the simulation only.** Their choices come from the event-driven IAUS and bounded HTN of §1, with the top contributors and margin logged as `motivated_by` edges. No LLM chooses an action, so no decision waits on a model, and Foresight rollouts and MAX-speed runs need no fallback policy. Deliberation consults, where a model picked from a legal-action menu, are retired by ADR-0034 (D27); they may return only as a research experiment after G3b, through a new ADR.
