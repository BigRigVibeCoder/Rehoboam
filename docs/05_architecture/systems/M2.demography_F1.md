## 1. How F1 is added without unfreezing rung 0

The rung-0 slice (0.2.0) is frozen and SPR-012/013 built against it. `lint_odd.py` keys specs by system id and
refuses a second file for one id, so a draft of the next version could not sit beside the frozen file. The
mechanism this PR adds is **next-version supersession** (`specs/systems/next/README.md`, `lint_odd.py --next`):
the draft lives in `specs/systems/next/`, supersedes the frozen file for the lint run (so M7's and T1's reads and
commands are checked against it), and the lint proves the frozen slice is preserved: every 0.2.0 column with its
entity, type, plane and fixed-point flag; every 0.2.0 `[algorithm.*]` table byte for byte; the 0.2.0 RNG purposes
as a prefix in order (the generated `RngPurpose` discriminants are positional); every 0.2.0 invariant, applied
command payload, schedule binding, emitted kind, parameter and default. A Registry delta (`registry_delta.toml`)
may replace M2's `[systems]` entry only by adding (the lint checks bounds and existing defaults are kept) and may
add verbs. **A frozen sentence changes only through an `[[amends]]` record** (REV-SPR-015 R1): the frozen text
byte for byte, the new text and a reason, both texts checked by the lint, every amendment printed as an `AMEND`
line. The draft amends four: INV-M2-03 (birth and commanded timers cross Month pulses), S2 (F1 compiles `tfr` and
`elite_fertility_mult`), EXTINCTION's `when` (newborns of the slot count as living) and the `[initialization]` text.
In `--next` mode the superseding spec is held to frozen-level strictness on `[[applies]]` and `[[emits]]`, a frozen
`from` may only grow, and a frozen `when`, `[initialization]` text or schedule step is kept inside the new text or
amended (R6). Ten planted changes are rejected by the lint's own self-check, and three positive controls are rendered
by `bin/check_all.sh` from the draft itself with `sed` (never copied, so they cannot drift: NIT-4).

What `lint_odd.py` accepted before this PR: one spec per id; `--extra-specs` (adds, refuses a repeated id);
`--specs` (replaces the whole set, with the smoke world's X-prefix rules); one `fidelity` level per spec; no
version, slice or profile mechanism. The alternative considered, profile-tagged sections inside one file, would
have meant editing the frozen file and teaching every consumer (`gen_skeletons.py`, `--rust`, `--brief`) about
profiles; rejected (65% that supersession is the better shape).

## 2. The F1 slice in one page

- **Births** (`[algorithm.fertility_f1]`). Married women aged 15 to 49 who are not pregnant and not within 11
  months of a birth draw once a month against a Prob32 conception probability per five-year band. The band
  values come from the Coale–Trussell (1974) natural-fertility schedule n(a) at level M = `tfr` / TFR1, where
  TFR1 = 9.3935 is the TFR the F1 marriage pattern would give with no mortality (so `tfr` keeps its Registry
  meaning). The monthly probability is c = b / (12 − 20 b) with b = M n(a) a year, so that a woman with 20 skipped
  pulses after each conception (9 gestation + 11 postpartum) bears exactly M n(a) children a year in the long run.
  A hit becomes a **birth timer** at a keyed day start of the ninth month after the conception month; the timer's
  payload is the conception pulse (the one fact a re-sample needs, since `resample_timer` sees no world: R2), and
  the pregnancy is state in `person.conceived_tick` and `person.unborn_father` (the husband at conception, so a
  posthumous child keeps him), which a re-sample never touches. At the fire, `apply_birth` issues
  `H64(mother, parity)` (rh-ids `person/child/v1`, with the deterministic rehash), draws the sex keyed by the
  child's own id alone (105 : 100; NIT-5), inserts the ledger row **through SPR-014's row insert** (every other owner's
  columns at their type's zero; the row is visible to M7's `on_birth` and T1 in the same instant), appends the
  life-course row with mother, father, sex and `birth_tick`, updates the mother's parity and last birth, and
  emits `BIRTH`. A mother compacted before the instant loses the child; a mother dying in the same slot still
  bears it, whatever the order of the two timers, and M2's own scans of the step count the newborn (MIN-3).
- **Partnering** (`[algorithm.partnering_f1]`). Unmarried, un-named women aged 15 to 49 (not in holy orders)
  draw monthly against a nuptiality probability per band (design values: mean age at first marriage 18.8, 99.7%
  ever married by 50, the early pattern). On a hit the husband is the argmin of a keyed scalar hash over the eligible men
  (alive, unmarried, un-named, not in holy orders, not kin within two degrees over the life-course log, preferably
  5 years younger to 15 years older), so one man more or less in the pool changes a match only when he is the winner
  (MIN-8), and a **marriage timer** at a keyed day start inside the month is set. From SPR-018 the candidates come
  from the bride's settlement only (`person.residence`, S3, read lagged with neutral 0 = no restriction; REV-SPR-018 R8:
  no residence moves in P1a, so no household spans two settlements). The same `apply_marry` applies M2's
  timers, commanded marriages and T1's `Marry` commands (frozen payload `{a, b, tick}`); M2's minimum ages bind M2's
  own draws only, a command follows its sender's age rule (MIN-5); a failed precondition drops and reports, never
  applies in part. Named actors are left to
  T1: `person.named` is read (lagged, neutral 0).
- **Households** (`[algorithm.households_f1]`). `household.*` rows (`H64(head, formation tick)` ids, sorted,
  never removed), membership in `person.household`. Formed by a marriage (the couple lives in the household the
  husband heads, else the wife's, else a new one), inherited by the surviving spouse or the oldest member, dissolved
  when empty. Genesis: manifest households (servants are members, MIN-6), one-person households for everyone else, and the
  manifest couple married with their children's parents set (so siblings never marry). `household.yaml` gains an
  unrelated founder so that a marriage is possible at rung 1 (R8).
- **At a death** (H4, after the frozen S12): the spouse is widowed and the marriage row closed, headship moves,
  pending births and marriages involving the dead are dropped when they fire.
- **Commands M2 accepts:** exactly `ScheduleDeath` and `Marry`, with the frozen payloads, drained by the Day/Vital
  binding (`drained_by`, REV-EVO-001 R2). A command dated later than the drain is **held** (`[algorithm.lifecycle_f1]`,
  shape (a) of EVO-001 R6): a `pending.*` row with every payload slot and a `COMMANDED_DEATH` or `COMMANDED_MARRIAGE`
  timer under the purpose `commanded` (`resample_on_fork = false`, draws nothing, keeps its tick on a re-sample), so a
  commanded death is never re-drawn as a hazard death and never loses its cause, culprit or `cause_event` (R5). The
  (b) variant, sender-owned timers, is one paragraph (L5). Divorce, adoption, legitimation, `ScheduleBirth` and
  household moves were considered and left to F2 or P1b.
- **RNG purposes, every one keyed per CLAUDE.md:** `death_time` (frozen), `conception` (woman, month),
  `marriage_time` (woman, month), `birth_sex` (child alone, t = 0), `commanded` (key person, tick; no draw). The
  first three are vital timers and `resample_on_fork`; `birth_sex` is not a timer and keeps a child's sex across
  branches; `commanded` only names the held outcomes' timers.
- **Invariants:** INV-M2-01, 02, 04, 05 unchanged; INV-M2-03 amended to death and marriage timers (BLK-1);
  INV-M2-06 (acyclic genealogy, parental-age bounds stated in calendar months, MIN-4), 07 (genealogical ids and
  parity), 08 (exclusive, symmetric, non-incestuous marriage), 09 (households), 10 (pregnancy state and its one
  timer, without a tick equality, R2), 11 (M2's own marriage timers), 12 (births conserved with the log and the
  events), 13 (held commands and their timers).
- **Registry delta:** verbs `BIRTH` (tellability 96) and `MARRIAGE` (112); defaults `tfr = 4.9` (R7: NRR = 1 at
  4.74 with widowhood; 4.9 sits at r = +0.11%/yr), `elite_fertility_mult = 1.0`. No new parameter: the nuptiality standard, the ages, the gestation and postpartum
  months and the sex ratio are compiled constants of the algorithm tables, like the Brass standard.
- **Tests:** V-SF-24 (conceptions per susceptible woman-month = c_i, the only exact binomial identity; the age
  shape of births is a report against the chain's values, R4) and V-SF-25 (nuptiality and fathers) are new rows in
  `docs/09`, marked "proposed (Q6)" and reported at G1a (R10); V-SF-01 (growth) is the acceptance test of the `tfr`
  default.

## 3. Kinship: what the effect language needs from M2's log, and that the log carries it

The effect language v0.5.0 (`specs/registry/effect_language_v0.md` §5.3, on PR #41, D37) defines its genealogy
prelude over **five M2 columns and one ledger flag**, and nothing else from M2:

| Relation (owner M2) | Reads |
|---|---|
| `children`, `kin`, `related`, `ancestors`, `descendants`, `descends`, `line`, `up`, `common` | `life_course.person_id`, `life_course.mother`, `life_course.father` |
| `line_agnatic`, `up_agnatic`, `common_agnatic` | `life_course.person_id`, `life_course.father` |
| `lineage` | the three above, `life_course.birth_tick`, and `person.alive` (pruning) |
| the law programs on a line | `life_course.birth_tick` (sibling order, ties by `person_id`), `life_course.sex` (cognatic eligibility) |

All five log columns exist in the frozen 0.2.0 and F1 writes every one of them **at entry for every birth**
(`apply_birth`, F9; REV-P1a NEW-7): `person_id` = `H64(mother, parity)`, `mother` ≠ 0 always, `father` = the
husband at conception (0 only for a founder or immigrant without one), `sex` from the keyed draw, `birth_tick` =
the birth instant. Two properties the language relies on hold by construction: the genealogy is acyclic with a
minimum parental age (INV-M2-06), and the children of one mother have distinct birth ticks (one pending birth per
woman, INV-M2-10), so the sibling order never needs the `person_id` tie-break for one mother. The log is
append-only and written only by M2 in `Vital`, so M7 and T1 read it `same_instant` (NEW-6) and a child born this
instant is already on every line at `Polity` and `Agenda`.

What the language does **not** take from M2 in v0, and F1 nevertheless provides: the living spouse
(`person.spouse`, read by T1 directly), the marriage log (a `spouses(p)` relation over `marriage.*` would be a
`needs_code` addition in `rh-registry`, not a language change) and households (`person.household`,
`household.head`, read by S6's inheritance of heirlooms and by M4 F0 in P1b). Bounds to note for SPR-017:
`children(p)` is bounded at 64, safely above F1's maximum of about 21 births per woman (ages 15 to 49, 20-month
closed intervals) and a man's remarriages; `kin(p, 6)` at 256 may truncate in a large family, which is the
language's `truncated_relations` diagnostic, not M2's concern. The effect language is not redefined here.

## 4. What SPR-016 (F1 mortality) will need, listed and not specified

1. **Fixed-point `exp` and `ln`** on `rh_core::fixed` (Q16_16 in, with the rounding stated and golden vectors from
   an exact reference), so that Brass runs at any (α, β): `Y(x) = α + β Ys(x)` and `l = 1 / (1 + exp(2Y))` instead
   of the β = 1 odds form; `ln` for `Ys` from the standard, or a precomputed `Ys` table in fixed point.
2. **The infant sub-schedule** below age 1 (Brass is weak there: `Ys(0) = −∞`; R12 §9). The red-team dossier taken
   in as a proposal (batch 05) offers Coale–Demeny West `q0` and `q1–4` as a lookup by `e0`; births make this
   matter: F1 births put newborns into the linear `l(0) → l(1)` interpolation of rung 0, which spreads the first
   year's deaths evenly instead of concentrating them in the first month.
3. **The old-age tail** above 90 (a Perks or Beard logistic, Brass p. 59): rung 0 forces death at 100.
4. **The density modifier** `q_a = 1 − (1 − q_a⁰)(y / y*)^{ε_m}` (R09 §2) with the entitlement hook stubbed at
   `y / y* = 1` until M4 F0 (P1.5a), and density N / K once M1 F0 gives K (SPR-018); the same hook on fertility
   (`ε_f`, `f_a = f_a⁰ (y / y*)^{ε_f}`) multiplies the level M of `[algorithm.fertility_f1]` F3, which is why F3
   keeps M a single Q16_16 factor.
5. **The era hook:** `medical_level` (S2, `[era_state]`) moving α (`05.1` §3.1), when S2 has an owner.
6. **Interaction with births, to keep:** `apply_death` H4 (widowhood, headship) must stay the single death path
   whatever hazard produced the death; a death in childbirth is ordered by F10 (the birth timer fires first).
   Maternal mortality is not in F1 and would be a new purpose, keyed by the mother and the birth instant.
