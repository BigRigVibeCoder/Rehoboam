# 05.2 — World Model, Hydration and Data Architecture

*Status: Draft v1.0 (2026-10-15). Sources: R01, R02, R06, R10. Decisions: ADR-0003, 0004, 0007, 0008. Machine-readable DDL: `specs/chronicle/schema.sql`.*

---

## 1. Identity model

| Kind | ID | How it is assigned | Immutable derived attributes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person | `PersonId(u64)` | **Birth serial**: births are processed in order `(tick, mother_id, parity)`; founders get `0..N₀−1` from population synthesis. IDs are never reused. | Face, voice, body, temperament jitter, given name (culture table plus `H`), handedness: all `H(world_seed, person_id, field)`. Never stored. |
| Region | `RegionId(u16)` | World-gen order | — |
| Settlement | `SettlementId(u32)` | World-gen, then founding order | Plan seed `H(world_seed, "burg", id)` |
| Household | `HouseholdId(u32)` | Formation order | — |
| Building / plot | `PlotId(u64)` | `H(settlement_seed, plot_index)` | Building variant, given (era, wealth, culture) |
| Named actor | Same `PersonId` (or `CreatureId`) | Promotion is a flag on the person's ledger row. Creatures (dragons) get ledger rows in a creature table. | — |
| Polity / faction / army | `PolityId`, `FactionId`, `ArmyId` | Creation order | — |
| Event | `(timeline_id, EventId(u64))` | `EventId = (day_index << 24) \| seq_in_day`. Ordered and deterministic. | — |
| Timeline | `TimelineId(u32)` | 0 = canonical; forks get new IDs | Parent and fork tick |

**Rule.** Any attribute that can be derived from `H(seed, id, field)` is derived, not stored. It is persisted only once it has been *revealed* (facts ledger, §6) or *changed by events* (state tables).

## 2. Spatial model: four nested layers

```
Raster (terrain, climate, biome, physical hazards)       512²–4096² cells; manifest-set resolution
  └─ Region graph (provinces + typed routes)              300–2,000 regions, Voronoi/Azgaar-style
      └─ Settlements (burgs) — abstract by default        N households, M workshops/shops, wards
          └─ Settlement plan (wards → streets → plots)    generated lazily + deterministically (rh-worldgen)
              └─ Buildings + interiors                    lazy; interiors only when entered
```

- **World generation** (P1) reimplements the algorithms from Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator (MIT) in Rust, as the crate `rh-worldgen`. The steps are:
  1. jittered grid and Voronoi tessellation;
  2. heightmap templates;
  3. temperature and precipitation;
  4. rivers by flux accumulation;
  5. biomes;
  6. cultures and burgs;
  7. state growth by priority flood fill;
  8. Urquhart-graph routes.

  Burg seeds follow FMG's convention, `map seed + burg id`, as precedent for the seed hierarchy (R06 §4.2).
- **Settlement plans** are part of `rh-worldgen` and are **shared by the server and the Godot client** (same crate, linked through gdext). Both sides therefore compute the same plot layout.
  - Plans are a function of `(settlement_seed, generator_version, sim snapshot: population, wealth, era, damage overrides)`.
  - Plans grow **incrementally**: new plots infill along existing roads, and the layout is never globally re-solved. A plot stays where it is between visits.
- Plots are **bound to simulation entities**: household residences, workshops, shops, temples and barracks. That binding is what makes errands "real": the baker's shop is a plot owned by an actual household with actual stock (M4 F2+).
- Simulation events that change places (fires, demolitions, new keeps) are stored as **overrides** over the generated baseline.

## 3. Hydration: tiers, rings and leases

### 3.1 Rings around the camera or avatar
Starting values, tuned per vantage:

| Ring | Radius | Tier | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interaction | Hydrate at 80 m, dehydrate at 130 m, plus ≥ 10 s out of view | T3 Embodied (≤ 200 hard cap) | Real persons with full components, navmesh, perception and barks |
| Conversation | Focused NPCs | T4 Conversant (1–3) | Dialogue slots |
| Crowd | ≤ 300–500 m | T2c crowd proxies | **Real `PersonId`s sampled without replacement** from ledger persons whose schedule places them outdoors there at that hour. Shown as VAT instances. |
| Far | ≤ 2 km | Impostors / aggregate | Density textures, lit windows, smoke, traffic streaks |
| Spectacle | Draw distance | T1 entities with a visual footprint | Dragons, armies, processions, fires |

### 3.2 Leases and lookahead decrees (temporal consistency)
1. **Lease.** When a person hydrates to T3, the bubble takes a **lease** on that person's fast state: position, current activity and short-term needs.
2. **Decrees.** While the person is leased, macro steps exclude their row from stochastic draws. Instead they issue **lookahead decrees** sampled from the same keyed RNG, for example "dies of plague at t = d14 h09" or "migrates on day 22".
3. **Enactment.** The bubble enacts each decree at time *t*, or the decree fires on dehydration if the person leaves first. Because a decree's outcome is identical whether or not anyone is watching, **observer invariance** holds (DET-6).
4. **Material events.** Anything that happens in the bubble and touches authoritative state is a *material* event and is journaled: a theft, a death caused by the player, a conversation that yields a speech act, a reveal. It applies at the next sync point. Cosmetic behaviour is never fed back.
5. **Catch-up.** A pinned T1 actor that has not been evaluated since tick t₀ is advanced lazily. Slow variables (ageing, wealth drift) use closed-form updates; only salient events are replayed from its agenda.

### 3.3 Pinning (promotion) policy
A person is **pinned** (sticky; facts persisted) when any of these is true:
- they were spoken to (T4);
- they were in T3 and in view for ≥ N s within M min;
- they were subject or object of a material event;
- they are referenced by a sift match;
- they hold an office;
- they were targeted by the Oracle;
- they are a first-degree relation referenced in dialogue. These are pinned as a **stub** (ID plus pinned facts), hydrated fully only when encountered. A **depth cap** on stubs prevents chain disaggregation.

Living persons are never un-pinned. Dead pinned persons are retained while they are referenced (§7).

### 3.4 What persists on dehydration

| State | Unpinned | Pinned |
|---|---|---|
| Derived traits (`H`) | Not stored | Not stored |
| Ledger row deltas (region, health, alive, wealth band) | Written back | Written back |
| Exact position and animation | Dropped (snapped to plot or anchor) | Dropped |
| Revealed backstory, relationships, dialogue summary, reputation with the player, inventory deltas, schedule overrides | n/a (revealing anything pins first) | Facts ledger |
| Unrevealed flavour | Dropped; regenerates identically from `H` | Dropped unless revealed |

### 3.5 Schedules, errands and boundary flux
1. **T0 schedule templates.** About 30–60 Markov activity-block templates keyed by occupation × age band × sex × season × faction. They are aggregated into hourly per-cell presence and origin–destination flows. These flows drive the crowd proxies and contagion contact rates.
2. **Concrete errands (T3).** Errands are generated from real household stocks, debts and summons, keyed `H(id, day, "errand")`. Their destinations are real plots with stock.
3. **Venue behaviour.** Places advertise need satisfaction in the style of The Sims "advertisements".
4. **Alibi generation.** When a T2c proxy is promoted to T3, its errand is sampled *conditioned on its observed heading and position* (Sunshine-Hill, R01 §1), so it never turns around unexpectedly.
5. **Boundary flux.** The inward crossing rate is ≈ density × speed / π × perimeter: about 1.3 entrants/s on a quiet street and 5/s in a busy market. The hydration budget is therefore 5–10 per second, with prefetch along the camera's velocity. Entrants are always existing proxies, never fresh spawns.

### 3.6 The six consistency criteria (acceptance tests)
1. **Identity stability.** The same ID gives the same immutable traits, regardless of when, where or in what order it is hydrated.
2. **Conservation.** Macro counts equal the sum of rows at every sync point.
3. **Observational non-contradiction.** Everything the player has seen or been told is canon (facts ledger).
4. **Temporal consistency.** One authority per entity per interval (leases).
5. **Distributional fidelity.** Hydrated samples are statistically indistinguishable from what full simulation would produce.
6. **Observer independence.** Hydration does not perturb the macro future; only material events do.

## 4. Event model (the chronicle)

The schema below follows R02 §R1 and R10 §R1. The DDL is in `specs/chronicle/schema.sql`.

| Table | Purpose | Key columns |
|---|---|---|
| `event` | One row per notable event | `timeline_id, event_id, tick, verb_id, act_class bitset, place_id, magnitude, tellability u8, secrecy {public, witnessed, faction, secret}, rule_id, rule_version, origin {sim, player, llm_choice, director}` |
| `event_role` | Participants | `event_id, entity_id, concrete_role (initiator/target/partner/bystander/instrument), abstract_role (authority/successor/kin/ally/rival/subordinate/member)` |
| `event_snapshot` | **Anchored state**: exactly the predicate values the rule and sifter read at emission | `event_id, entity_id, attr_id, value i32` |
| `causal_edge` | Typed causality | `cause_id, effect_id, kind {contingent, triggered, relayed_knowledge, motivated_by, enabled, probabilistic, player, director}, weight, evidence` |
| `knowledge` | Who knows what (notable events and actors only) | `entity_id, event_id, acquired_tick, via_event_id, channel, fidelity, variant_id, salience` |
| `collection` | Event collections (war → campaign → battle; plague → wave) | `collection_id, parent_id, type, members` |
| `aggregate_event` | Mass events (a famine killing 40,000) | `event_id, counts by (age band, sex, class, region), distributions` |
| `state_interval` | SCD2 slowly changing facts | `entity_id, attr_id, value, valid_from, valid_to` |
| `sig_component` | Retroactive significance | `event_id, component, value, computed_at_tick` |
| `sift_match` | Pattern instances | `match_id, pattern_id, pattern_version, bindings, event_ids, status {partial, complete, expired}, opened_tick, closed_tick, score` |
| `facts_ledger` | Revealed facts about persons and places | `subject_id, fact_type, value, source_event_id, revealed_tick` |
| `input_journal` | All nondeterministic inputs | `timeline_id, tick, seq, kind, payload, payload_hash, producer (player / llm job id / manifest)` |
| `state_hash` | Determinism ledger | `timeline_id, tick, xxh3_64` |
| `keyframe` | Snapshot index | `timeline_id, tick, path, state_hash, bytes, schema_version` |
| `regional_aggregate` | Commoner-level telemetry | `timeline_id, region_id, month, metric_id, value` |

**What gets an `event` row.** Events of a tellable act class, events involving a named or pinned participant, events with rarity above a threshold, and every material bubble event. Commoner life is recorded as regional aggregates and `aggregate_event`s. Per-person rows are written only for promoted persons (R10 §R1).

**Recording causality** (R02 §R2):
- **Contingency** edges are written only from explicit unlocking:
  - an action references a past action (revenge *for* X);
  - a queued follow-up is later performed;
  - knowledge is relayed;
  - a player or Oracle action.
- Utility decisions record their **top contributors and margin** as `motivated_by` edges. These are explanation, not contingency.
- The Registry declares, per action, which past-event roles the action can reference. This makes **contingent unlocking** something authors design rather than an accident.

## 5. Material vs cosmetic bubble activity

| Activity | Class | Journaled? |
|---|---|---|
| Walking, idling, barks, animation, ambient chatter | Cosmetic | No |
| NPC dialogue prose | Cosmetic (prose) | No; only the extracted speech acts below are journaled |
| Extracted speech acts (INFORM, ACCUSE, THREATEN, PROMISE, CONFESS) | **Material** | Yes |
| Player theft, violence, gifts, purchases | **Material** | Yes |
| Decree enactment (deaths, births, migrations) | Already authoritative (macro) | Already in the journal through the seed |
| Revealed facts (displaying a person's bio) | Pinning and facts only; no state change | `facts_ledger` |

## 6. Facts ledger

Every fact displayed to you about a person or place, whether in the console, the 3D client or dialogue, is written to `facts_ledger` with its source event. Later generation (dialogue, bios, narration) must treat these facts as **T0 canon**, the same tier as simulation events. Together with pinning, this is what makes the world *inhabitable* (S2).

## 7. Storage tiers and retention

| Tier | Contents | Technology | Budget (1,000 years) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hot** (sim RAM) | Live tables, sifter partial matches, last ~30 sim-days of events as Arrow batches, spatial grid | Rust structures | 0.5–2 GB at 1M persons |
| **Warm** | One immutable Parquet segment per timeline per sim-year: events, roles, snapshots, edges, aggregates, intervals, matches | Chronicle Writer (single writer); DuckDB **read-only** views over the glob | ≈ 3 GB chronicle at 5k named actors (≈ 11 B/event); aggregates 0.2–2.5 GB |
| **Cold** | Per-century recompressed segments (zstd-19); archive partitions of low-salience events | Parquet | Similar or smaller |
| **Keyframes** | Arrow IPC + zstd-3, permanent every 10 sim-years; yearly LRU near the cursor | rh-snapshot | ≈ 0.2 GB per keyframe at 1M persons (≈ 20 GB per 1,000 years); ≈ 2 GB at 100k |
| **Journal** | Inputs, hashes, keyframe index | SQLite WAL | Small |

**Compaction** (replaces the draft's "delete weight < 7"). At each century boundary:
1. recompute significance components (`05.4` §3);
2. pin ancestors of significant events;
3. fold unpinned old events into `collection` summary nodes with counts, preserving base rates;
4. move them to archive partitions and rewire causal edges to the nearest retained ancestor.

Hard deletion is an explicit, opt-in disk-pressure action that runs only on cold archives that the simulation never reads back.

## 8. Snapshots, replay, timelines

- **Keyframe format.** Arrow IPC, one record batch per table, schema-versioned. Raster fields use u16 quantisation plus delta encoding (R03: 8.4 MB → 2.1 MB).
- **Seek** (drop in at tick *t*): load the nearest keyframe ≤ *t*, then replay with the journal. A yearly cache around the cursor keeps seeks ≤ 10 s at 100k persons.
- **Scrub:** reads the chronicle and `regional_aggregate` only. Never re-simulates.
- **Fork:** create a new `timeline_id` with (parent, fork_tick). Parquet segments before the fork are shared by reference; journal inputs after the fork belong to the child.
- **Schema evolution.** Arrow schemas are versioned. Old keyframes either load through migrations or are regenerated from journal + seed with pinned model versions (DET-10).

## 9. Data-volume summary (order of magnitude)

| Item | 100k persons | 1M persons |

| Ledger in RAM | ~6 MB | ~64 MB |

| Chronicle, 1,000 years (named-actor events) | ~1–3 GB | ~3–5 GB |
| Keyframes, 10-year cadence | ~2 GB | ~20 GB |
