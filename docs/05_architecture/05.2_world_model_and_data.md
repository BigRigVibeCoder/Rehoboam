## 0. The three planes (ADR-0024)

Every piece of state belongs to exactly one plane. Plane-A code never reads planes B or C. The only way into A is a **journaled material input**.

| Plane | What lives there | Hash |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Authoritative** | Ledger (the living), life-course log, regions, polities, markets, named actors (T1), beliefs, agenda and timers, chronicle **events** | `state_hash` |
| **B: Observation** | Pins, facts ledger, hydrated presentation state (positions, animation, barks), view caches, camera | `observation_hash` |
| **C: Narrative** | Sift matches, significance, retention and visibility tiers, ratings, chronicles and prose | `narrative_hash` (a pure function of A's events plus versions) |

## 1. Identity model (ADR-0026)

| Kind | ID | How it is assigned | Derived attributes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person | `PersonId(u64)` | **Genealogical, content-addressed**. A child is `H64(mother_id, parity)`, i.e. "the same mother's n-th child" in every branch. A founder is `H64(seed, "founder", k)`. An immigrant or off-map materialisation is `H64(seed, "immigrant", origin, t, k)`. Collisions are checked at birth and resolved by a deterministic rehash. IDs are never reused. | Face, voice, body, temperament jitter, given name (culture table plus `H`), handedness: all `H(world_seed, person_id, field)`. Never stored. |
| Region | `RegionId(u16)` | World-gen order | — |
| Settlement | `SettlementId(u32)` | World-gen, then founding order | Plan seed `H(world_seed, "burg", id)` |
| Household | `HouseholdId(u64)` | `H(founding member id, formation tick)` | — |
| Building / plot | `PlotId(u64)` | `H(settlement_seed, plot_index)` | Building variant, given (era, wealth, culture) |
| Named actor (T1) | Same `PersonId` (or `CreatureId`) | Promotion is a plane-A flag, set **only** by simulation criteria (office, great-house rank, material involvement, Registry notability) or a journaled input. **Never by pinning.** | — |
| Polity / faction / army | `PolityId`, `FactionId`, `ArmyId` | Creation order | — |
| Event | `(timeline_id, EventId(u64))` | One counter per timeline, from 0 (`rh-events`, SPR-006). Order = emission order = `(tick, phase, seq)`. **No back-dating** (ADR-0025). The id carries no date: the day is `tick.div_euclid(1440)`, read from the event's `tick`. | Sift match | `(timeline_id, MatchId(u64))` | A separate ID space in plane C. Never interleaved with EventIds. | — |
| Timeline | `TimelineId(u32)` | 0 = canonical; forks get new IDs | Parent, fork tick, salt lineage |

**Time.** `SimTime = i64` minutes from the world epoch. Negative values are allowed, so founders can be born before year 0.

**Flavour taxonomy** (what may be said about a person):

| Kind | Source | Plane | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inert flavour** | `H(seed, id, "bio", k)`, conditioned on the life-course log | Presentation. B once shown. | Turn of phrase, a childhood anecdote with no simulation consequence |
| **Behavioural flavour** | `H(seed, id, field)` that plane-A systems also read | A | Favourite tavern, chosen among taverns in their ward. The errand and venue systems read the same attribute, so the 3D world never contradicts the bio. |
| **Historical claims** | Life-course log and chronicle only, stated *as of* a tick | A | "Lost two siblings in the plague of 398; moved to the Ward of Ash after the famine" |

## 2. Spatial model: four nested layers

```
Raster (terrain, climate, biome, physical hazards)       512²–4096² cells; manifest-set resolution
  └─ Region graph (provinces + typed routes)              300–2,000 regions, Voronoi/Azgaar-style
      └─ Settlements (burgs) — abstract by default        N households, M workshops/shops, wards
          └─ Settlement plan (wards → streets → plots)    generated lazily + deterministically (rh-worldgen)
              └─ Buildings + interiors                    lazy; interiors only when entered
```

- **World generation** (P1) reimplements the algorithms from Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator (MIT) in the Rust crate `rh-worldgen`:
  1. Jittered grid and Voronoi.
  2. Heightmap templates.
  3. Temperature and precipitation.
  4. Rivers by flux accumulation.
  5. Biomes.
  6. Cultures and burgs.
  7. State growth by priority flood fill.
  8. Urquhart-graph routes.

  Burg seeds follow FMG's convention, `map seed + burg id`, as the precedent for the seed hierarchy (R06 §4.2).
- **Settlement plans** are part of `rh-worldgen` and are **shared by the server and the Godot client**.
  - A plan is a function of `(settlement_seed, generator_version, plane-A snapshot: population, wealth, era, damage overrides)`.
  - Plans grow **incrementally**: new plots infill along existing roads, and plans are never re-solved globally.
- **Plots are bound to simulation entities**: residences, workshops, shops, temples, barracks. Concrete errands that use plot-level stock need M4 at F2 (household stocks). See WP P5.0 in `11`.
- **Simulation events that change places** (fires, demolitions, new keeps) are stored as plane-A **overrides** over the generated baseline.

## 3. Hydration: a view, never an authority

### 3.1 Rings around the camera or avatar
Starting values, tuned per vantage:

| Ring | Radius | Tier | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interaction | Hydrate at 80 m, dehydrate at 130 m, plus ≥ 10 s out of view | T3 Embodied (≤ 200 hard cap) | Real persons, presented: navmesh motion, animation, barks |
| Conversation | Focused NPCs | T4 Conversant (1–3) | Dialogue slots |
| Crowd | ≤ 300–500 m | T2c crowd proxies | **Real `PersonId`s sampled without replacement** from living persons whose schedule places them outdoors there at that hour. Shown as VAT instances. |
| Far | ≤ 2 km | Impostors / aggregate | Density textures, lit windows, smoke, traffic streaks |
| Spectacle | Draw distance | T1 actors or SoA objects (armies) with a world-scale footprint | Dragons, armies, processions, fires |

**When the bubble exists.** T3/T4 hydration exists only at **Live** speed, where the street clock runs at 20–60:1 sim:real (R01 §R5.4). Any faster speed band collapses the bubble.

### 3.2 Scheduled outcomes, not leases (ADR-0025)
1. At each pulse, systems sample intra-pulse **outcome times** for every affected person, observed or not. Outcomes include deaths, births, illness onset, migrations and marriages. Each outcome becomes a **timer** in the plane-A agenda and fires at its own tick in every mode. The bubble only *displays* outcomes.
2. Hydrated persons' positions and activities are **views** computed from schedule templates plus scheduled outcomes. **Authority never moves to the bubble.**
3. **Material inputs** are player actions, player speech acts and Oracle verbs.
   - Each is journaled with a **dependency scope**: person, household, settlement or region.
   - In the `Inputs` phase, pending timers in that scope are cancelled and re-sampled with key `H(seed, branch_salt, purpose, entity, t_input, epoch)`.
   - Precedence: the earlier tick wins; at equal ticks, inputs apply first.
4. **Bubble NPCs never originate material events.** Their behaviour, including what they say, is a deterministic function of plane A plus your journaled inputs. The LLM only realises the prose (05.3 §8).
5. **Acting in the past forks.** A material input at a tick before the timeline head creates a fork at that tick. Dropping in to watch or inspect is read-only.

### 3.3 Pinning (plane B)
A person is **pinned** when you engage with them:
- you speak to them;
- they are in view for ≥ N s;
- you open their inspector;
- the Oracle targets them;
- they are referenced in dialogue you heard.

A pin guarantees **presentation consistency**: facts you were shown are kept in the facts ledger, and every later presentation (dialogue, bios, narration) must honour them. Pins **do not** change plane A. They don't promote to T1, don't create events, don't retain rows, and don't alter scheduling.

### 3.4 What persists after you look away

| State | Where |
|---|---|
| Derived traits (`H`) | Not stored; recomputed |
| Life events, residence, kinship | Plane A (life-course log); always present for everyone |
| Exact position and animation | Dropped (it was a view) |
| Facts you were shown (inert flavour, dialogue content) | Plane B facts ledger |
| Anything you *did* (material inputs) | Plane A, via the journal |

### 3.5 Schedules, errands and boundary flux
1. **Schedule templates.** About 30–60 Markov activity-block templates keyed by occupation × age band × sex × season × faction. They are aggregated into hourly per-cell presence and origin–destination flows. These flows drive T2c crowds and contagion contact rates (plane A for contagion; views for crowds). *(ADR-0031: these templates are `S5.daily_life`'s activity function. Outside focus regions its output is plane B, shown but not consequential; inside a focus region or a micro world at `blocks` or `minutes` it is authoritative. Viewer and engine call the same function, so raising a region's resolution never changes how it looks.)*
2. **Concrete errands (T3 view).** Derived from household stock, debts and summons (`H(id, day, "errand")`), with destinations that are real plots with stock. Requires M4 F2.
3. **Venues** advertise need satisfaction (Sims-style). Behavioural flavour such as a favourite tavern biases the choice.
4. **Alibi generation.** When a T2c proxy is promoted to T3, its presented errand is sampled conditioned on its observed heading and position. Because errands are views, this never affects plane A.
5. **Boundary flux.** About 1.3 entrants/s on a quiet street and about 5/s in a busy market. The hydration budget is 5–10/s, with prefetch along the camera's velocity.

### 3.6 Consistency criteria (acceptance tests, `09` §2.5)
1. **Identity stability.** The same ID gives the same traits in any order, at any time and on any machine.
2. **Conservation.** Macro counts equal the sum of living rows at every pulse.
3. **Observational non-contradiction.** Plane-B facts are honoured by every later presentation.
4. **Temporal consistency.** Scheduled outcomes fire at the same tick watched or unwatched.
5. **Distributional fidelity.** Presented crowds match plane-A schedules. This is checked with an equivalence test (TOST), not a "fail to reject" test.
6. **Observer independence.** `state_hash` is unchanged by any amount of viewing, pinning, inspecting or hydrating (DET-6, binding from P2).

**What gets an `event` row** is decided by plane-A criteria only:
- an act class that is tellable;
- a named (T1) participant;
- rarity above a threshold;
- any material input.

Pins do **not** create event rows. Commoner life appears as regional aggregates, `aggregate_event`s and the life-course log.

### 4.0 Event-row admission (DATA-5): the rule

*Status: **Proposed, awaits the director (D38).** Architect, SPR-013 (2026-10-16; PR #37 Q8, PR #45 with its review). It fixes DATA-5's first criterion, "a tellable act class", as **tellable at the world's scale**. DATA-5's second sentence (commoner life as aggregates and the life-course log) then holds only above the ceiling, which amends DATA-5 rather than clarifying it (70%); D38 decides. P1.0 needs none of it: P1.0 admits every draft (below). `[event_rows]` and the admission flag arrive with P1.9, the Chronicle Writer (`docs/11`).*

**Where the decision is made.** Admission is a plane-A **flag** computed once, in `rh-core`, at the level merge that assigns EventIds (`05.1` §2 rule 4), from plane-A state alone. **Every draft gets an EventId**, admitted or not: the counter, the running event-log hash and `DraftRef` resolution do not depend on the flag. They cannot, because plane-A state stores EventIds (S6's `chain_head`, the `knowledge` and `diffusion_front` keys, `QueuedCommand.cause`), and skipping a refused draft would resolve the next `DraftRef` to the next admitted event: a deterministic wrong causal link. Plane A never branches on the flag. Only admitted events get an `EVENT` record in channel A and a chronicle row. A refused event is counted per verb and reason (V-DATA-07's audit), and whatever its system wrote to state (the life-course exit, the ledger row) stands, so the life-course log and the aggregates carry commoner life, as DATA-5 asks. A cause edge from an admitted event to a refused one keeps the refused id, and the edge row says whether its cause is told: `causal_edge.cause_told`, added to channel A v0 and the canonical form v0 by SPR-020, before any build wrote a segment (an in-place amendment: `specs/chronicle/envelope_v0.md` §11, REV-SPR-020 R6); the writer computes it from its admission bitset, and every P1a cause is told.

**P1.0 admits every draft.** SPR-011 ruling 11 (every death at rungs 0–2 is told) is implemented as the absence of a rule: every emitted draft is an `EVENT` record, on `solo`, `household`, `hamlet` and the 10,000-founder fixture of V-SIFT-01 alike, `EXTINCTION` included (V-SCALE-04). The rule below admits everything on those worlds too (all are under the ceiling), so its arrival at P1.9 changes no P1a channel-A content.

A draft is admitted when **any** of the four criteria holds:

| # | Criterion (DATA-5) | How plane A decides, with integers only |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A tellable act class** | The Registry's `[event_rows]` gives each act class a population ceiling, `tellable_up_to` (persons, u32). The class is tellable in sim-year *y* when `living_at_start(y)`, the ledger's row count at the Year pulse, is at or below the ceiling: a world that grows past it stops telling commoner acts from the next year, and one that shrinks under it resumes. A verb is admitted when any of its act classes is tellable. A class absent from the table has ceiling 0 (never by this route); `4294967295` means always. **Verbs with tellability 255, the world-ending ones (`EXTINCTION`), are admitted whatever the population.** P1a design value: **10,000 for every act class**, so that every P1a world is fully told, the 10,000-founder fixture and the P1.14 dynastic world included; the ceiling first bites at the kingdom worlds (P1.11) and `val_khor`. One value per class so that calibration at G1a (the director's family histories) can move them one at a time |
| 2 | **A named (T1) participant** | Any role row whose entity is a person with `person.named = 1` (T1's column, `05.3`). When T1 is not built the column does not exist and nobody is named |
| 3 | **Rarity above a threshold** | A measured base rate, not a declared one. Per (verb, rarity key), `rh-core` keeps the count of occurrences (admitted or not) of the current and the previous completed sim-year in a core table, `rh-core/event_rates`, in `state_hash` and in keyframes. An occurrence in sim-year *y* is admitted when the previous year's rate was under the verb's threshold: `count(y − 1) × 100,000 < rare_below × living_at_start(y − 1)`, with `rare_below` the verb's Registry integer (occurrences per 100k person-years) and the population the one that year started with. The previous year is used so that admission within a year does not depend on the order of that year's events; the route is off in sim-year 0 and for a verb without `rare_below`. The rarity key is a payload slot the verb declares as an enum (`rarity_key`; `DEATH`: slot 0, the cause enum), so a murder is rare where a natural death is not. The table is registered only when a Registry verb declares `rare_below`: the plane hash covers every registered table by name (`bin/gen_hash_golden.py`'s layout), so a table that always existed, even empty, would move every baseline, and one registered only when needed moves none. **Deferred to P1.14** |
| 4 | **A material input** | `origin` ∈ {player, oracle, director}, or a draft that is the consequence of a journaled input (ADR-0025). This needs the input's origin carried through timers and lifecycle commands: a timer re-sampled or set in the `Inputs` phase, and a command it sends, fire later with the input's origin. That plumbing is `specs/chronicle/envelope_v0.md` §2 (SPR-020; REV-SPR-020 R13, R14): the job's origin, inherited by every timer, command and draft the job creates and hash-neutral at `sim`; the journal's input kinds map to origins there (an `OracleMove` is `oracle` whoever produced it) |

Aggregate verbs (`aggregate = true`, e.g. `FAMINE_DECLARED`) are always admitted: one row per region and period is already the summary DATA-5 asks for.

**How it scales through P1a.** P1.3 adds births and marriages (`birth`, `marry`), P1.7 successions (`succeed_to`, `depose`, `claim`, `lapse`). Under the rule nothing changes per verb: every P1a world is under the ceiling, so every birth, marriage, death and succession is a row, and the P1.14 dynastic world (10k) is fully told. At the kingdom worlds (P1.11, up to 50k) and above, a commoner's birth, marriage and death go to the life-course log and the aggregates while a named actor's go to `event` rows (criterion 2), a succession always has a named party, and the rare commoner event (a murder, an execution) comes back through criterion 3. The expected volume at 50k is then a few thousand rows a year, all story material for the sifter (`08` §1 assumes 21k named events a month at 1M). V-PLN-05 holds because no criterion reads plane B.

**Registry and schema changes (C2, with P1.9).** A new typed section:

```toml
[event_rows]                        # DATA-5 criterion 1 (05.2 §4.0): the population ceiling per act class
death      = { tellable_up_to = 10000 }
birth      = { tellable_up_to = 10000 }
marry      = { tellable_up_to = 10000 }
# ... every act class, one line each (the P1a design value is 10000 for all)
```

`Registry::problems` refuses a key that is not in `[act_classes].ids`; `lint_odd.py` and `manifest_v0.py` are unaffected (`[act_classes].ids` keeps its list order, so act-class bit positions do not move). `causal_edge` gains `cause_told BOOLEAN NOT NULL` (in channel A v0 and canonical v0 since SPR-020). Criterion 3 adds two optional verb fields at P1.14: `rare_below` (u32, per 100k person-years) and `rarity_key` (the index of an enum payload slot). No manifest field changes: the manifest selects nothing here (ADR-0010), and the living count is engine state, so `WorldSpec` needs nothing.

**For the director (D38).** DATA-5 says commoner life shall be recorded as aggregates and the life-course log; under this rule that holds only above the ceiling, and below it commoner deaths, births and marriages are also `event` rows, as ruling 11 (D30) already does for rungs 0–2. The Architect and the independent reviewer read this as an amendment of DATA-5, not a clarification (70%). **Recommendation:** approve the amendment and record it against DATA-5 in `docs/03`. The alternative is a ceiling of 0 for `death`, `birth` and `marry`, which keeps DATA-5's sentence literally and makes ruling 11 a standing P1.0 exception. **Confidence 65%.**

**Alternatives considered.**
- *A threshold on `verb.tellability`* (admit when ≥ 128): no new schema, but it conflates admission with the significance prior, and no single threshold both admits every death at rung 0 (ruling 11) and keeps commoner deaths out at 1M (`DEATH` is 128 everywhere). Rejected. 40%.
- *A manifest-selected policy* (`narrative.event_rows = everyone | named`, Registry-defined options): the same effect, chosen per world rather than by population. Rejected because it adds a knob the director must set for every world and lets two worlds of the same size have different histories for a reason that is not simulation; the ladder already fixes scale by population. 50%.
- *A boolean per act class* (PR #37's recommendation): the simplest schema, but `death = true` contradicts DATA-5 at 1M and `death = false` contradicts ruling 11 at rung 0, so ruling 11 would have to live on as a per-world exception. The ceiling is that boolean with the scale made explicit. 55% for the boolean alone.
- *The ceiling against the manifest's compiled population* (this proposal's first draft): static per world and part of `manifest_hash`, but the ledger moves: a world that grows past the ceiling would keep telling every commoner act, and the rarity test needs the living count anyway. Replaced by `living_at_start(y)` (PR #45 review, m4).
- *Refusing a draft at the merge* (this proposal's first draft): no EventId for a refused draft. Rejected by the review (B1): plane-A state stores EventIds and `DraftRef` resolution would shift. Replaced by the flag.

**Recording causality** (R02 §R2):
- **Contingency edges** come only from explicit unlocking: an action references a past action, a queued follow-up is performed, knowledge is relayed, or a player/Oracle action occurs.
- **Utility decisions** record their top contributors and margin as `motivated_by` edges.
- **The Registry declares, per verb, which past-event roles it can reference.** This is how contingent unlocking is designed in.

### 4.1a The cinematic event envelope (ADR-0035 §7a)

Downstream tools (the storyboard generator, the audio exporter, the local saga reader) need more than an actor, a target and a verb. Every chronicle-worthy event carries five groups of facts. **The engine only engines applies to the envelope too:** channel A stores only *simulated* facts; anything purely presentational is derived downstream in plane C from those facts.

| Group | Stored in channel A (plane A) | Where | Derived downstream (plane C, `05.11`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Cast | Actor, target and witnesses | `event_role` (already the design) | Visual DNA (`05.11` §5) |
| 2. Staging | Place id and tick (already stored); **cell coordinates** where the world tracks cells; **weather** once `M3.agriculture` simulates it | `event`; `event_snapshot` attributes `cell_x`, `cell_y`, `weather` | Time of day from the tick and calendar; lighting from time of day, place type and weather; the acoustic from the place type (Registry `[place_types]`) |
| 3. Emotional vector | The actor's and target's emotional state at that moment, where minds simulate emotion: PAD mood and the strongest OCC emotion, fixed point | `event_snapshot` attributes `emotion_pleasure`, `emotion_arousal`, `emotion_dominance`, `emotion_occ`, `emotion_intensity` | Where it is not simulated (pops, minor actors), a template inference from the act class, labelled as inferred |
| 4. Action and props | The verb; the **instrument**: the `S6.artifacts` id when an artifact was used; the instrument's or cell's **contaminant bitflags** (Registry `[contaminants]`: blood, ash, poison, …) at the event | `event.verb_id`; `event_role` with role `instrument`; `event_snapshot` attribute `contaminants` | Prop status ("blood dripping", "glowing") from the verb and outcome |
| 5. Causal "why" | **Typed motive edges** to the inciting event: `RETALIATION_FOR`, `JEALOUSY_OF`, `VOW_OF`, `AMBITION_FOR`, `FEAR_OF`, `LOYALTY_TO`, `GRIEF_FOR`, `GREED_FOR`, `CORRUPTION_BY` (Registry `[motive_edges]`) | `causal_edge` with a motive kind | Narrative phrasing |

- **Budget.** About 35 bytes per event beyond what the event already holds: weather 1 byte, two actors' emotion snapshots (3 × i16 PAD plus an OCC code and intensity each) 16 bytes, the instrument 8 bytes, and a motive edge (kind plus the inciting `EventId`) 9 bytes, before compression. Where cells and contaminants are tracked, add about 8 bytes (cell coordinates 4, contaminant flags 2 per marked entity), about 43 in all. V-SAGA-15 measures it.
- **Anchored reads.** Every envelope attribute is declared in the Registry `[attributes]`, so patterns read it as `attr@eN` (for example `emotion_occ(A)@e2 == anger`), and the SQL twin reads the same snapshot.
- **Only what is simulated is stored.** No weather before M3 simulates it; no emotion for actors whose minds do not simulate it. A missing field is absent, never guessed in plane A.
- **When it lands.** WP P1.9 (P1a). P0's event schema (SPR-006, `EventDraft`) must leave room for roles, anchored snapshots and typed edges; SPR-006 is not changed (BCK-001 §2). **The contract** (SPR-020): `specs/chronicle/envelope_v0.md` fixes the role vocabulary, the `EventDraft` shape, the anchored snapshot rule, the edge kinds with motive, weight and evidence, what enters `state_hash`, the provenance rule and the plane-B index, with `schema.sql`, `channel_a_v0.sql` and `canonical_v0.md` as its machine-readable twins.
- **Why it matters.** Every tragedy points to its origin through the explicit causal DAG, so the downstream tools never have to guess a motive.

**Provenance of change: the "Legends reverse key"** (SAGA-15; ADR-0035 §4a). State that records damage or transformation stores its cause EventId **inside the state value**: `Ruin { cause: EventId }` on a building, `Scar { cause: EventId, site }` on a person, a captured or cursed artifact (`artifact.condition_cause`), a burned field. Every such state resolves to an existing, earlier event (V-SAGA-21). "What last happened here?" for an arbitrary place is answered by a **plane-B chronicle index**: DuckDB over the Parquet chronicle by `place_id`, `cell` or entity. There is **no** `last_mutating_event_id` column on every tile.

**No spontaneous props** (SAGA-12). Every item in an event's roles or envelope already exists: an artifact has its origin event and bearer chain (`S6.artifacts`); a mundane prop points to the acquisition event that drew it from an owner's stock (M4). No event uses an item before its origin event (V-SAGA-17).

### 4.1 Knowledge at scale (RT-M7)
Per-actor knowledge rows for every public event would be about 37.5M rows a year at 5k actors, roughly 0.2–0.3 TB per 1,000 years. Knowledge is therefore split:

| Knowledge kind | Representation |
|---|---|
| Witnessed, faction-internal, secret events; testimony hops between named actors | Per-actor `knowledge` rows |
| Public, crowd and document knowledge | `diffusion_front` arrival times per (event, region), evaluated on demand: `knows(a, e, t) = row(a, e) OR (public(e) AND arrival(region(a, t), e) ≤ t)` |
| Pop-level belief shares | Proposition **templates** with lifecycle and garbage collection: shares below ε for longer than τ are dropped |
| Suspicion posteriors | **Sparse**: only pairs above prior + δ, or pairs flagged by an active scheme or investigation |

## 6. Storage tiers and retention

| Tier | Contents | Technology | Budget (1,000 years) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hot** (sim RAM) | Live tables, agenda and timers, last ~30 sim-days of events as Arrow batches, spatial grid | Rust structures | See `08` §5 |
| **Warm** | Immutable Parquet segments per timeline and sim-year: events, roles, snapshots, edges, knowledge, diffusion fronts, aggregates, intervals, life-course | Chronicle Writer (single writer); DuckDB **read-only** views | See `08` §6 (re-baselined: tens of GB at 1M) |
| **Cold** | Per-century recompressed segments (zstd-19) | Parquet | Similar or smaller |
| **Keyframes** | Arrow IPC + zstd-3. Permanent every 10 sim-years, plus a yearly LRU near the cursor | rh-snapshot | Measured in P1 (`08`) |
| **Journal** | Inputs, hashes, keyframe index, engine-build references | SQLite WAL | Small |

**Retention.** Named-actor events are **never physically compacted**: they are cheap, and physical compaction conflicts with forks that share segments by reference.
- **Low-salience events** are hidden behind a per-timeline **visibility sidecar** (plane C), not moved or rewritten.
- **Rarity base rates** are kept as counts per (pattern, era) in plane C.
- **Hard deletion** is an explicit, opt-in disk-pressure action on cold archives only. It never touches data plane A reads, or data that cannot be regenerated by the archived engine build (ADR-0007, RT-M3).

## 7. Snapshots, replay, timelines

- **Keyframe format.** Arrow IPC, one record batch per table, schema-versioned and tagged with the `engine_build` that wrote it. Raster fields are quantised to u16 and delta-coded (R03). P0 (SPR-007 T-02) stores every table as `(table, row)` pairs of its canonical row encoding under one fixed schema, in batches of at most 65,536 rows, with a header table instead of Arrow schema metadata (a hash map, which plane A does not name); the format is `rehoboam/rh-snapshot/keyframe/v1` (`05.1` §6 item 6). Typed per-table batches are for when tables have typed columns to carry.
- **Seek / drop in.** Load the nearest keyframe at or before *t*, then replay the journal. Read-only (ADR-0025).
- **Scrub.** Reads the chronicle and `regional_aggregate` only. Never re-simulates.
- **Fork.** Creates a new `timeline_id` with `(parent, fork_tick)` and extends the **salt lineage** (05.1 §6.9). Parquet segments before the fork are shared by reference. Journal inputs after the fork belong to the child.
- **Engine upgrades: "frozen past, migrated future"** (DET-10).
  - Each world records the content-addressed **engine build** of every segment of history, and those builds are archived.
  - An upgrade writes a **migration keyframe** at the head.
  - History before it replays with the archived build, or is read from the chronicle.
  - Keyframes are never thinned below what an archived build can regenerate.
