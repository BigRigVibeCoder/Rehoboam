> **BLUF:** Do not approve as is. **1 blocker, 9 major, 12 minor.** The blocker: both specs make their "same instant" promises (immediate succession, grief at the death) through Day-pulse bindings that the shipped engine does not run at a death instant, because SPR-011 puts every death timer at a keyed minute inside the month and `rh-core` fires bindings only at pulse instants; M7's arbitration self-timer "at (now, Polity)" is refused outright by the agenda's no-back-dating rule. As written, V-SAGA-19 and INV-M7-01 fail at every death and grief never fires. The fix is small (deaths at day-start instants; arbitrate inside `on_death` as `exit` already does) and belongs to SPR-011 ruling 7 and the M2 spec, not to a later EVO. The majors are contract and plan gaps that would stop or mislead SPR-021 to SPR-025: a `previous_pulse` read that poisons `Engine::new` until BCK-002 A-28, which the plan now puts in P1b; reads of owners no P1a world registers; fixed-point widths that zero out the low-bound murder hazards; an undeclared `ScheduleDeath` schema that lets a "murdered" ruler reign for up to a month; no birth step in either spec; IAUS considerations and curves defined nowhere; two missing sprint dependencies; and a G1a gate on a band the spec itself says P1a may not reach. The sources check out: every hazard ratio and bound in M7 matches R12 §1–2, with one derived number (the 0.03 non-lethal deposition rate) that should be labelled a design derivation.

## 3. Blocker and major findings

### BLK-1. The same-instant mechanisms cannot run on the shipped engine as the specs and SPR-011 are written

**What the specs promise.** M7 binds `Day`/`Polity` with `when = "only at instants where a death timer fired in Vital or an M7 timer is due in Polity"` (`M7.dynasty.odd.toml:381-385`), and `on_death` says "schedule the arbitration timer at (now, Polity) of this same instant (SAGA-13)" (`:530-532`). INV-M7-01 requires that `awaiting` never survives a tick boundary (`:564-567`) and INV-M7-02 that every titleholder death is followed in the same instant by `SUCCEED` or `INTERREGNUM_CRISIS` (`:568-571`). T1 binds `Day`/`Vital` for grief with `when = "only at instants where a death timer fires"` (`T1.minds.odd.toml:573-577`), and its `grief` submodel reads the instant's dead (`:709-712`).

**What the engine does.** The engine runs an instant whenever a bound pulse fires, a timer is due or an input is queued (`crates/rh-core/src/engine.rs:5-6`), but in that instant only the bindings whose pulse fires run: `run_instant` filters the binding pass by `fired.contains(&n.pulse)` (`engine.rs:731-735`, `:799`). At an instant that is not a day boundary, `fired` is empty and only the timer pass runs. A `when` clause in a spec is documentation; nothing in `rh-core` reads it. Separately, the engine "refuses a timer that is not strictly after the current `(instant, phase)`" (`crates/rh-core/src/agenda.rs:18-20`, ADR-0025 item 2), so a timer at `(now, Polity)` scheduled from a `Polity` step is refused.

**What the plan does.** SPR-011 ruling 7 and T-07 put each death "at a keyed minute inside the month (k = 1), never at the pulse instant" (`SPR-011_WalkingSkeleton.md:35`, `:153`). The M2 spec agrees: "intra-month death times as timers" (`M2.demography.odd.toml:101`, `scales.temporal`).

**Consequence.** Every death fires at a minute that is not a day boundary. M2's timer pass runs `apply_death` there; T1's `Day`/`Vital` grief binding and M7's `Day`/`Polity` `on_death` binding do not run until the next day boundary. So: a sovereign's title keeps a dead `holder` until the next day's `Polity` (INV-M7-01 and V-SAGA-19 fail at every death), grief is never credited (the dead row is compacted at `Bookkeeping` of the death instant under SPR-011 ruling 5, `:33`, so by the next day `alive = 0, death_tick = now` matches nothing), and the one mechanism M7 names for "the same instant", a self-timer at `(now, Polity)`, is refused by the agenda. The Architect's round-1 ruling "every death takes effect as an M2 death timer in Vital" (M7 header `:9`; BCK-002 A-30, `BCK-002_ArchitectBacklog.md:50`) is necessary but not sufficient: it fixes the phase, not the instant. SPR-011 ruling 6 (`:34`) defers "same-instant delivery to a later phase" to SPR-014, and PRJ-002 §6 lists that EVO as "Missing" (`PRJ-002_P1aPlan.md:149`), but as described ("delivery of a command to its owner at a later phase") it still delivers to an owner whose binding does not run at that instant. The director would be approving a spec whose central P1a gate behaviour has no mechanism in the engine or in any sprint.

**Recommended fix** (two small changes, both in material the Architect owns, no engine change):
1. **Deaths fire at day-start instants.** Amend SPR-011 ruling 7 and T-07 and the M2 `death_time` purpose: `k = 1` draws the *day* inside the month, and the timer is at `(that day's first minute, Vital)`. Nothing in P1a needs minute resolution; V-DET-12 only needs tick ≥ instant and a timer in the agenda; M7's own deposition timers are already day-resolved ("day 2 to the last", `M7:411-414`). Then M2's timer pass, T1's `Day`/`Vital` binding and M7's `Day`/`Polity` binding all run in the death instant, in that order, with no new engine machinery. State in both specs that "the death instant" is a day boundary.
2. **Arbitrate inside `on_death`,** exactly as `exit` already does ("run arbitrate in the same step", `M7:538-541`). Delete the `(now, Polity)` self-timer. Interregnum resolution and regency end keep their future-dated timers.
3. Record in SPR-014 that same-instant command delivery is then needed only for commands whose owner has a binding at that instant, and say so in the EVO.

If the Architect wants minute-level deaths kept, the alternative is an engine change in SPR-014 (a lifecycle command delivered to a later phase of the same instant triggers the owner's step at that instant even off-pulse), which is C3 scheduler semantics and should be ruled before the specs are approved. Confidence **9/10**.

### MAJ-1. A `previous_pulse` read leaves the engine poisoned until A-28, which the plan moves to P1b

M7 reads `person.named` at `previous_pulse` (`M7:344-348`) and also `vow.vower`, `vow.status` and `polity.exec_constraint` at `previous_pulse` (`:349-363`). T1 reads `vow.vower`, `vow.conflict` and `artifact.rumour_reach` at `previous_pulse` (`T1:531-555`). `Engine::new` refuses any `previous_pulse` read with `SimError::Unsupported` "from the start" (`engine.rs:242-243`, `unsupported_lag` at `:1172-1189`), per BCK-002 A-28 (`BCK-002:48`). PRJ-002 §8 puts A-28 in "SPR-031 (S5); before S6 and S7 in P1b" (`PRJ-002:187`), and ruling M-4 moves SPR-031 to P1b (`:171`). So SPR-021 would register M7 (the test passes) and then fail to construct an engine. The PR's registration test does not reach `Engine::new`.

**Fix.** `person.named` is a flag, not a rate: read it `lagged` (start of instant; identical in value for a yearly dormancy pass). Declare the S6, S7 and M6 reads only when those owners are registered (see MAJ-2), or read them `lagged` too: for a monthly rate read at a `Month` binding, "previous pulse" and "start of this instant" are the same value. Alternatively move A-28 into SPR-014, which is the scheduler sprint anyway. Confidence **9/10**.

### MAJ-2. Reads of owners no P1a world registers, and what the plan says registration does

M7 reads `polity.exec_constraint` from `M6.polity` (`M7:359-363`) and says it is "held at the manifest's reference value until M6-lite" (`:39`, `:597`). T1 reads five S6/S7 columns (`T1:531-555`). In a P1a world none of M6, S6, S7 is registered. `register.rs:251-253` *reports* a read of an unregistered owner (the PR's test asserts exactly that report, `real_specs.rs:773-777`); only a *send* to an unregistered owner is refused (`register.rs:478`). At run time the column does not exist, so a step that reads it fails, and no one owns "the manifest's reference value". PRJ-002 §6 provides "owners or neutral stand-ins" for S5's reads only (`PRJ-002:161`), and ruling M-4's rationale, "registration refuses a read without one" (`:171`), is not what the shipped code does. The M-4 conclusion still stands (S5 cannot *run* in P1a), but the reason should be stated correctly, and M7 and T1 have the same gap.

**Fix.** State in both specs that `access()` declares a read only when the world registers its owner (the rule the PR already applies to commands, decision 9), and that the coupling's `neutral` value applies when it is absent: for M7, `m_ec = 1` (`exec_constraint = 0`, `M7:601`); for T1, `s_vow = 0`, seek consideration as at `rumour_reach = 0`. Correct M-4's wording. Confidence **8/10**.

### MAJ-3. Fixed-point widths for the exit probabilities, and an undeclared dependency on fixed-point `exp`

`exit_hazards` draws against `p = 1 - exp(-h/12)` "in fixed point" (`M7:403-410`, `:526-529`) without saying which type. The generated `Params` are all `Fixed` (Q16_16; `crates/rh-core/tests/generated/M7_dynasty.rs`), and `rh_core::fixed` has no finer probability than Q16_16 until SPR-011 T-01 adds `Prob32` (SPR-011 ruling 4, `:32`, which makes this same argument for M2). At the Registry's lower corner, `murder_hazard_base = 0.001 × primogeniture_hr 0.2 × exec_constraint_hr 0.3` gives `h_m = 6 × 10⁻⁵` per year and `5 × 10⁻⁶` per month, which is 0.33 of a Q16_16 step: it rounds to zero, so a primogeniture realm with an assembly can never see a murder, and INV-M7-04's lower bound (`:577-579`) is unreachable. Even the mid-range (`0.01 × 0.25 × 0.5` per year, `1.04 × 10⁻⁴` per month) carries a 3% quantisation error. Second, `exp` is a transcendental; in plane A it exists only as SPR-016's fixed-point `exp`/`ln` (`PRJ-002:37`), and SPR-022 depends on SPR-021 only (`:43`). The wave order happens to put SPR-016 before SPR-022, but the dependency is not declared and the spec does not name the function.

**Fix.** Compute the yearly hazard in Q16_16 and the monthly probability as `Prob32` (ruling 4's type), and state every width and rounding as SPR-011 ruling 7 does for M2. Use `p = h/12` directly: for `h ≤ 0.3` per year the error against `1 − exp(−h/12)` is below `p²/2`, under 0.03% relative, which the V-SF-06 Poisson bands cannot see, and it removes the transcendental. If `exp` is kept, add SPR-016 to SPR-022's dependencies. Confidence **9/10** on the quantisation, **8/10** that `h/12` is the right call.

### MAJ-4. `ScheduleDeath` has no owner-declared schema, the senders disagree, and a successful attempt kills nobody for up to a month

M7 sends `ScheduleDeath {ruler, tick, cause = murder, culprit, cause = the hazard draw}` (`M7:287-290`); T1 sends `{victim, cause = murder, culprit, cause_event}` with no tick (`T1:408-411`). The M2 proposal commit adds the columns the two specs read but declares neither `ScheduleDeath` nor `Marry` as commands M2 applies, and keeps `interaction = "None at rung 0"` (`M2:117`); its only mention is inside the `death_cause` meaning (`M2:76-82`). M2's only binding is `Month`/`Vital` (`M2:99-101`), and "the owner receives its queue when it next runs" (`05.1` §2 rule 2). So T1's `MURDER_ATTEMPT`, which "records the outcome (success or failure) in the event" (`T1:385-386`), is followed by the victim reigning, deciding and being read as alive for up to 30 days until M2's next month binding applies the command at a tick nobody specified. The chronicle then reads "murdered" before "died", and M7's arbitration follows the death, not the attempt. M7's own one-month-ahead draw (`:528`) is consistent with the monthly binding, but only because it chose to be.

**Fix.** The M2 spec declares the commands it applies, with one schema: `ScheduleDeath {victim, tick, cause, culprit, cause_event}` and `Marry {a, b, cause_event}`. T1 sets `tick` to the next day boundary at the earliest (ADR-0025: no back-dating; a same-day death would need BLK-1's instant rule). M2 gains a `Day`/`Vital` binding that drains its queue (SPR-014's scope), so a command is applied the next day, not the next month. State the attempt-to-death delay in `MURDER_ATTEMPT`'s `when`. Confidence **8/10**.

### MAJ-5. No birth step: houses and relation edges are set only at genesis or promotion

M7's `person.house` is "Set at birth from the parent the house's law follows" (`M7:71-77`), but M7 has no step that runs at a birth: its bindings are `Day`/`Polity`, `Month`/`Polity`, `Day`/`Agenda`, `Year`/`Polity` (`:381-400`), none with a birth step, and births are M2 timers in `Vital` (SPR-015). T1 builds relation edges "from M2's genealogy" at world creation (`T1:635-637`) and on `Promote` (`:721-724`), and claims "a named actor grieves an unnamed child" (`:176-182`), but no step adds a `child` edge when a named actor's child is born, nor a `sibling` edge to its named siblings, nor a `spouse` edge when M2 applies `Marry`. `grief` reads only "an unended relation row" (`:711`), so a parent named before a child's birth never grieves that child, and `kin_other` edges to later-born nephews never exist. Dormancy's "within dormancy_degrees" (`M7:558-561`) also depends on `person.house` being set for the newborn.

**Fix.** Either an `on_birth` step in M7 (house from the law's parent) and in T1 (parent, child, sibling, grandparent, kin_other edges; `spouse` on `Marry`), bound where births fire (which BLK-1 makes a day boundary), or compute kinship from M2's `mother`/`father`/`spouse` columns at read time and keep `relation.*` only for the non-genealogical kinds (liege, vassal, ended edges). The second is simpler and cannot drift from the ledger. Confidence **9/10**.

### MAJ-6. IAUS considerations and response curves are defined nowhere, and no sprint owns them

`[decision_types]` lists about twenty consideration names (`age_gap`, `partner_tier`, `kin_distance_to_granter`, `claim_kind`, `holder_is_minor`, `rival_claim`, …; `registry_v0.toml:302-311`) with the comment "the curve tables land with the effect language, P1.15" (`:303-304`). T1's `decide` scores "the product of the type's fixed-point response curves over its considerations … times the compensation factor" (`T1:733-736`). No file defines what any consideration computes, its domain, or a single curve. SPR-017's deliverables are "the typed, total evaluator; succession-law eligibility and ordering; verb effects as queued commands; golden test" (`PRJ-002:38`), nothing about considerations or curves, and SPR-024's spec list is "`T1.minds` (missing)" (`:45`). V-COG-10 ("top contributors and margin", `docs/09:283`) and V-COG-04 cannot be written against undefined inputs, and ADR-0010's "no free-string semantics" is breached in spirit: these are strings whose meaning only the Rust will hold.

**Fix.** Before SPR-024: a Registry section (`[considerations]`: input, domain, owner column it reads) and a curve table per decision type (`[response_curves]`: fixed-point lookup tables, `05.1` §4.3 item 2), typed under A-24, and the T1 spec's `decide` naming them. Give the deliverable to SPR-017 (the evaluator must read them anyway) or to SPR-024 explicitly. Confidence **9/10**.

### MAJ-7. SPR-024 lacks a dependency on SPR-021

SPR-024 (IAUS for `marry`, `seek_office`, `press_claim`, `feud`) depends on SPR-023 and SPR-017 only (`PRJ-002:45`, graph `:81-82`). `seek_office` sends `PetitionSeat` and `press_claim` sends `PressClaim` to M7 (`T1:413-419`), and decision 9 / registration ruling 5 refuse a send to an unregistered owner (`register.rs:478`); the candidate sets of every type come from "the actor's court" defined by M7's titles (`T1:630`). So SPR-024 cannot register, let alone pass V-COG-03/04, without SPR-021 merged. In the wave table they run in parallel (waves 6–7, `:120-121`).

**Fix.** Add `SPR-021 → SPR-024`, or split SPR-024 so that `marry` and `feud` (M2 only) land first and the two M7-coupled types follow SPR-021. Confidence **9/10**.

### MAJ-8. G1a gates a band that the spec declares on a P1b ensemble and says P1a may not reach

T1 declares V-SAGA-16's band "on the kingdom ensemble" (`T1:30`), which is the P1b rung-4 world (ADR-0035 §5; `docs/11`). `not_yet` says "The P1a break rate may fall below 0.5 per 100 actor-years" (`:41`), and the isolation `expect` says it is "reported (with the shortfall stated) under P1a sources alone" (`:862`). `docs/09` §5 gates G1a on V-SAGA-16 (`docs/09:693`), and PRJ-002 §5 repeats it ("Envelope and stress … V-SAGA-16 | SPR-025", `PRJ-002:139`). Ruling M-7 defines P1a variants for four other tests and does not mention V-SAGA-16 (`:174`). The director would approve a gate criterion that the spec under the same approval expects to fail.

**Fix.** Split V-SAGA-16 as the spec already splits its patterns (`T1:29-30`): the accounting identity and the exactly-one-timer rule gate G1a (they are checkable on the isolation fixture); the break band is a P1a report on the dynastic fixture world and gates G1b, under M-7, with the "one G1a list" (D34) edited to say so. Confidence **8/10**.

### MAJ-9. Traits "keyed by person only" are also keyed by the instant and the epoch

`trait_draw` is "keyed by person only, so a re-promoted actor gets the same traits" (`T1:590-593`); `promote` says "a later Promote recreates the same traits" (`:723`); `restart` says traits "are also recomputable from the trait_draw purpose" (`:561`). `StepCtx::rng` draws from the stream at `self.t`, the current instant (`crates/rh-core/src/system.rs:374-383`), and from SPR-011 T-02 (A-29) at the entity's epoch. A re-promotion at a later tick, or after any material input on the person, therefore draws different traits, and INV-T1-05 ("trait and ambition columns never change after a mind is created", `:763-766`) would be broken by the system's own `Promote`. The spec also says the mind's rows are kept on `Demote` (`:723`), so there is no reason to redraw at all.

**Fix.** On re-promotion reuse the kept `mind.*` rows and never redraw; draw traits once, at the first `Promote` or at genesis, and say the draw is keyed by that instant (so the restart claim becomes "restored from the keyframe", not "recomputable"). Also state whether a re-promotion resets `stress` and the three accounting columns together, or INV-T1-01's identity (`:747-750`) breaks at the second promotion. Confidence **8/10**.

### 7.2 New findings

**NEW-1 (major). Derived kinship from the live ledger loses every relation that passes through a dead person.** T1 now derives parents, children, spouse, siblings, grandparents, grandchildren and `kin_other` "from M2's mother, father and spouse through the bounded relation kin(2)" (`T1:180-181`, `:733`), and the `kin_distance_to_granter` consideration walks `kin(6)` over the same columns (`registry_v0.toml:326`). M7's legal heir is "effect-language eligibility and order over kin(n)" (`M7:564`), its regent "the nearest adult kin in the law's order" (`:564`), its dormancy criterion "farther than dormancy_degrees from every holder" (`:538`, `:588`), and its regrant candidates "kin of the liege's holder within dormancy_degrees" (`:580`). All of these read `person.mother`, `person.father` and `person.spouse`, which exist only on the live ledger: "the live ledger holds the living only; dead rows leave immediately" (ADR-0026 item 2), by core compaction at `Bookkeeping` of the death instant (SPR-011 ruling 5, `SPR-011:33`). A path of length 2 or more through a dead person is then unreadable: an actor's grandmother through a dead mother (the mother's row, with its `mother` id, is gone), first cousins through dead parents, a nephew through a dead sibling, and, for M7, the whole agnatic line once the previous generation has died, which is the normal case at a succession. The spec text claims the opposite ("cannot drift from the ledger and covers kin born after promotion", `T1:181`). The genealogy of everyone ever born is the life-course log (ADR-0026 item 3), which SPR-011 ruling 5 gives to M2 as `life_course.*` columns; neither spec reads it and M2's spec does not yet declare it (no `life_course` in `M2.demography.odd.toml` at `1cc0536`).

*Fix.* M2 declares `life_course.id`, `life_course.mother` and `life_course.father` (append-only, every person ever born; the walking skeleton writes them anyway, SPR-011 T-03) and T1 and M7 read those at `lagged` for every `kin(n)` walk, keeping `person.spouse` for the living spouse. Say which reverse index (children by parent id) each reader keeps and that it is rebuilt from the log at restore, so `restart` stays true. Confidence **9/10**.

**NEW-2 (minor). The same-month window drops day 1 from every hazard.** `exit_hazards` draws on day 1 for "days 2 to the last" with `n = days_in_month − 1` (`M7:552`, `:425`), so the realised hazard per ruler-year is `h × (D − 12)/D`, 3.3% below `h`. The isolation `expect` still requires "murders per 1,000 ruler-years at the reference state equal 1000 × murder_hazard_base × L within Poisson error" (`M7:682`): a systematic 3.3% shortfall, inside the noise of one run but not of the 32-seed ensemble. SPR-011 ruling 7's "never the Month-pulse instant itself" gives M2's natural deaths the same 1/30 shortfall per month unless T-05's monthly probability is scaled to the 29-day window; that is SPR-012's reference to state. *Fix:* either use `n = days_in_month` with `p` scaled to the full month and let a day-1 hit fall on day 2 (a one-day shift, no bias), or state the `(D − 12)/D` factor in the `expect` and in T-05. Confidence 8/10.

**NEW-3 (minor). Two "contract" functions are implementations.** `claim_rank` reads "succession_rank(actor, claimed title) by the title's law (M7 contract)" (`registry_v0.toml:328`) and grief uses "kin(2) of M2's contract" (`T1:733`). `05.1` §2 rule 1 limits contract crates to "types and accessors only, no implementation"; evaluating a succession law or walking a genealogy is neither. *Fix:* M7 owns a `claim.rank` column (set when the claim is created, refreshed in `regrant` and `on_death`), and `kin(n)` is the effect-language relation `05.5` §2.1 already declares, evaluated by `rh-registry` over NEW-1's log columns. Confidence 8/10.

**NEW-4 (minor). "`access()` declares a read only when its owner is registered" has no home in code.** `SystemModel::access(&self)` takes no world (`05.1` §2), so each system would need the registered set at construction. One rule in `rh-core` is cleaner: registration drops a declared read or send whose owner is absent and reports it, and the step view supplies the coupling's neutral value, so the specs need not repeat the rule. *Fix:* put it in SPR-014's scope (the scheduler sprint) and cite it from both specs. Confidence 7/10.

M2's own text still says minutes.** `scales.temporal` ("intra-month death times as timers", `M2:105`) and `death_time`'s `use` (`:121`) predate BLK-1; the `[[applies]]` block says day starts (`:84-87`).
